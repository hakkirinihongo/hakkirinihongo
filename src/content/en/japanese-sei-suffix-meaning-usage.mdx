What does the Japanese suffix ～性 (せい, sei) mean, and how is it used?

At first glance, *～性* appears to turn a word into an abstract noun denoting a quality or condition: *可能* *“possible”* becomes *可能性* *“possibility.”* But this equivalence is only partial. 

In reality, *～性* does not merely create a “quality”; rather, it introduces the idea of an **observable**, **classifiable**, and **analyzable property**, often in formal, technical, or academic contexts.
There are also other cases in which ～性 expresses a tendency, an ability, or a performance characteristic; in still others, it classifies what follows as belonging to a particular type, origin, or field.

It appears in seemingly different words such as 可能性 “possibility,” 安全性 “safety,” 人間性 “human nature,” and 植物性タンパク質 “plant-based proteins”: the same element occurs in all of them, but 性 does not contribute to the meaning of each word in exactly the same way.


For this reason, treating ～性 as simply equivalent to the English suffix *“-ity”* is useful only as a rough first approximation. The correspondence works well in words such as 可能性 *“possibility”* or 具体性 “concreteness”, but breaks down with expressions such as 植物性タンパク質, 神経性便秘, or 耐水性.

Here, 性 does not simply denote an abstract quality: it marks, respectively, an origin-based classification, a specialized cause or underlying nature, and a functional property that can be tested and assessed.

In modern Japanese, ～性 is in fact **a productive derivational element** that presents the preceding base as a **property**, **a state**, **a tendency**, **an ability**, or a **criterion of classification**.

The words formed in this way often designate characteristics that can be observed, compared, measured, increased, or reduced: one can speak of a *high probability*, verify the *safety* of a product, improve *productivity*, or assess the *reproducibility* of an experiment.

This function accounts for the high frequency of ～性 in scientific, technical, medical, administrative, and academic language. The suffix, however, is not confined to specialist registers: words such as 可能性, 必要性, and 人間性 are also part of the general vocabulary. What changes above all is the density with which formations in ～性 are used and the degree of specialization of the base to which the suffix attaches.

Its present-day behavior also reflects its historical development. 性 did not originate as a simple grammatical ending, but as a lexical element connected with the ideas of **nature**, **character**, and **innate disposition**. 

From this semantic core, its suffixal use gradually developed and became especially productive in the formation of modern vocabulary. Even in the most abstract or technical words, the fundamental idea of *“being X by nature”* or *“possessing the property X”* therefore remains recognizable.

In the following sections, we will examine the meaning and grammatical function of ～性, the bases it can combine with, and the main types of concepts it can form, before considering why not every word ending in 性 is automatically a productive instance of the suffix ～せい.






## How the Japanese Suffix ～性 (せい, sei) Works


In contemporary Japanese, *-性* is primarily a **derivational suffix** that forms **abstract nouns** and, in very general terms, expresses **property, state, tendency, degree, or character**.

Dictionaries describe it as an element added to a noun to indicate **“the quality or tendency”** of something, and it is normally read *セイ*. 

Historically, however, 性 was not originally a suffix in the strict sense: it also existed as an independent word meaning **“nature, character, disposition,”** and its use as a productive word-formation device became firmly established mainly between the late Edo period and the Meiji era, particularly in scientific and philosophical vocabulary.

In English, -性 often corresponds to the suffix **“-ity”** (for example, *可能* *“possible”* becomes *可能性* *“possibility”*); however, for intermediate and advanced learners of Japanese, the crucial point is this: **-性 is not simply equivalent to “-ity,”** but tends to form **a noun denoting a property**, often used in written, technical, institutional, or academic registers.

It should be distinguished from *-的*, which mainly forms an adjectival or relational qualifier, and from *-さ / -み*, which primarily nominalize adjectives. It should also be distinguished from 質 and 性質, which remain closer to full-fledged lexical nouns or established lexical units than to simple productive suffixes.

## Definition and Origin

At the lexical level, *性* has several layers of meaning. As an independent noun, it can indicate **innate nature**, **sex**, or even **grammatical gender**; as a suffix, by contrast, dictionaries describe it as an element that attaches to a nominal base to express **“the quality or tendency of something,”** or, more broadly, **“property, state, or degree.”**

This dual nature is important because it explains many apparent ambiguities: not every word ending in 性 is in fact a fully transparent instance of suffixal derivation.

From an etymological standpoint, the character 性 is composed of 心 and 生, with the original sense of *“that which is born in the heart,”* namely an **innate natural disposition**. This etymological profile helps explain why the suffix continues to preserve a strong semantic core connected with intrinsic nature, even when it occurs in modern and highly abstract words.

Historically, the use of *-性* as a productive suffix was not yet fully established in Classical Japanese. Its use as a word-formation mechanism spread primarily from the late Edo period through the Meiji era, particularly through translations of scientific and philosophical texts.

Historical research also suggests that Japanese played an active role in this process of grammaticalization, and that it was therefore not simply an imitation of modern Chinese.
In other words, *-性* did not originate as a purely grammatical mechanism, but as a **lexical concept** that gradually became grammaticalized.

## Grammatical Function and Combination Rules
From a morphological perspective, *-性* is a **postposed** element: it comes after a base and helps determine the grammatical class of the entire formation. Put simply, the result is a noun in most cases. 

This is a typical property of derivational suffixes: the suffix does not merely add meaning, but also determines the grammatical behavior of the derived form.

The literature on Sino-Japanese bases distinguishes several types of bases with which *-性* can combine: **nominal bases** *(体言系)*, **adjectival or predicative Sino-Japanese bases** *(相言系)*, **nominalized verbal bases or サ変-type bases** *(用言系)*, as well as more marginal cases such as **adverbial bases** *(副言系)* and less autonomous forms, sometimes described as **combining forms** *(結合形態)*.

In modern Japanese, *-性* is not limited to a single type of base: Sino-Japanese suffixes, including *性*, **occur predominantly with Sino-Japanese bases**, but they can also combine with native words and loanwords, provided the combination is attested and lexicalized.

This compatibility does not imply that any base can freely take *-性*. In general, however, formations in -性 are generally more natural with Sino-Japanese bases, particularly when the base lends itself to interpretation as a **classifiable property**.

Syntactically, words derived with *-性* can appear either as **independent nouns** or as **attributive elements**.

In the first case, the derived form clearly functions as a noun: *可能性がある, 具体性が足りない*. In the second, it modifies a following noun: *熱帯性海水魚, 植物性タンパク, 神経性便秘*; in some cases, it also appears in the structure **X性のN**, as in *金属性の調理器具*.

A crucial point is that not all words in *-性* display the same degree of freedom in these two uses. 
A nearly complementary distribution is often observed: forms such as 芸術性 and 国民性 appear mainly as independent nouns, whereas 熱帯性, 金属性, and 植物性 have a strong attributive tendency; other cases, such as 人間性, are more flexible, although they are still subject to certain pragmatic or stylistic restrictions.

As for pronunciation, the normal reading is セイ. The kanji, however, also has the reading ショウ, which survives in older or more lexicalized words.

## Semantic Range and Types of Concepts Expressed by ～性


The semantic core of *-性* remains remarkably stable: the suffix introduces the idea of **“being X by nature,” “having the trait X,” “displaying the property X.”** 

This persistence of meaning—also highlighted by studies that contrast -性 with -的 (to which we will return in a future article)—helps explain why words in -性 **often feel less “empty”** or less purely relational than other formations.


In brief: **-性 tends to name a property**, whereas **-的 tends to characterize something in terms of a domain or category, or by indicating resemblance**.
For learning purposes, it is useful to group the meanings into several recurring conceptual families.

This classification is a teaching-oriented synthesis, but it is consistent with the sources: dictionaries generally refer to **quality / tendency / state / degree**, while descriptive studies show that in practice -性 is used both to form **independent abstract nouns and classifying attributes**, with scientific, medical, and technical vocabulary particularly prominent in attributive uses.

One very useful observation is that words in -性 often denote properties that can be **measured, assessed, increased, reduced, confirmed, or improved**.

This is why collocations such as *安全性を高める*, *再現性が高い*, and *可能性を検討する* are extremely common. This collocational profile is one reason -性 is so well suited to academic argumentation and to scientific and administrative writing. This can be inferred both from the semantic descriptions found in dictionaries and from its prominence in the **Balanced Corpus of Contemporary Written Japanese**.

## Examples

The following sentences are designed to illustrate the main uses of -性: independent abstract nouns, technical attributive uses, and differences in register.

*<p class="jp-example">この製品の安全性は長期試験で確認された。</p>* *“The safety of this product was confirmed through long-term testing.”* *Usage note: a highly typical use of -性 as an assessable abstract noun in a technical or institutional register.* 
*<p class="jp-example">その計画には実現可能性があるが、予算面の課題が残っている。</p>* *“That plan is feasible, but budget-related issues remain.”* *Usage note: this illustrates a longer compound in which 可能性 is the nominal head.* 
*<p class="jp-example">植物性たんぱく質を中心に献立を組み立てた。</p>* *“I planned the menu mainly around plant-based proteins.”* *Usage note: an example of classifying attributive usage; here 性 does not correspond straightforwardly to “-ity”; instead, it means “plant-based” or “of plant origin.”* 
*<p class="jp-example">彼の発言には攻撃性が感じられる。</p>* *“There is a sense of aggression in his remarks.”* *Usage note: here -性 expresses a tendency/disposition.* 
*<p class="jp-example">この実験は再現性が高く、教育目的にも向いている。</p>* *“This experiment is highly reproducible and is also suitable for educational purposes.”* *Usage note: 再現性 is very common in academic and scientific language.* 
*<p class="jp-example">説明は丁寧だったが、具体性に欠けていた。</p>*  *“The explanation was thorough, but it lacked specificity.”* *Usage note: an excellent example of a Sino-Japanese adjectival base becoming a noun denoting a quality.*


*<p class="jp-example">新素材は耐水性と耐熱性の両方に優れている。</p>*  *“The new material excels in both water resistance and heat resistance.”* *Usage note: a highly productive use in descriptions of performance and technical specifications.* 

*<p class="jp-example">彼女は心配性だから、出発前に三回も切符を確認した。</p>* *“She is a natural worrier, so she checked her ticket no fewer than three times before leaving.”* *Usage note: 心配性 is a special case in which 性 is read しょう rather than せい.* 

*<p class="jp-example">人間性を一つの文化だけで説明するのは危険だ。</p>* *“It is dangerous to explain human nature in terms of a single culture.”* *Usage note: 人間性 functions primarily as an independent noun, rather than merely as an attribute.*


*<p class="jp-example">急性細菌性髄膜炎のように、病名では「-性」が重なることがある。</p>* *“As in ‘acute bacterial meningitis,’ the suffix ‘-性’ may appear more than once in the names of diseases.”* *Usage note: a useful sentence for recognizing the layered use of the suffix in specialist medical terminology.*

## Conclusions

As we have seen, -性 is especially characteristic of **written**, **formal**, **technical**, **administrative**, and **academic Japanese**. This does not mean, however, that it is absent from everyday speech: words such as 可能性 and 必要性 are obvious counterexamples, but formations in -性 are especially dense in technical and scientific registers.

The most important exceptions are not “errors,” but boundary cases. The first concerns the difference between an independent noun and a genuinely suffixal use. In *女性*, *男性*, *異性*, *性別*, and *性教育*, the character 性 belongs to a semantic field connected with sex/gender, not merely to the property-forming suffix. 

The second concerns the difference between the reading *セイ* and the reading *ショウ*. The kanji has both readings; in the modern derivational pattern examined here, *セイ* predominates, but numerous uses of *ショウ* or related readings remain: *本性* *(ほんしょう)*, *心配性* *(しんぱいしょう)*, *凝り性* *(こりしょう)*, *冷え性* *(ひえしょう)*. 

This means that **“a word ending in 性” is not automatically an instance of “the suffix -sei.”** 


To understand the meaning and use of *-性* properly, the most effective method is not to memorize isolated lists, but to organize its uses into **two patterns**. In the first, it functions as an **independent abstract noun**: *X性がある*, *X性が高い*, *X性を高める*. 
The second is the **attributive** pattern: *X性N* or *X性のN*.

A second practical recommendation is to keep two separate categories in mind from the outset: **productive** -セイ and **lexicalized** -ショウ (see the example sentence). The first includes words such as *可能性*, *安全性*, *具体性*, and *再現性*; the second, words such as *心配性*, *凝り性*, *冷え性*, and *本性*. From a teaching perspective, this is a highly effective distinction because it prevents incorrect readings and avoids overextending the suffix *-性* to every word ending in *性*.
