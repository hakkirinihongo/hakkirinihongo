What does the ～め (me) suffix mean in Japanese, and how is it used with adjectives?

The ～め suffix makes it possible to **express a variation in degree relative to what is considered normal, usual, or expected in a given situation**. 大きめ can describe something relatively large, 少なめ a slightly smaller amount, and 早め a time that is earlier than usual or expected. 

These forms allow speakers to talk about size, portions, timing, and intensity without having to specify an exact measurement: a slightly larger size, a smaller portion, a slightly earlier departure.

To understand their meaning, it is necessary to identify the point of comparison, which is often left implicit. A shirt described as 大きめ may be roomier than the size someone normally wears without being exceptionally large; asking for a 少なめ portion means opting for an amount smaller than what is normally served. 

The reference point may be a concrete object, a scheduled time, a habit, or a shared expectation. When we use the term “deviation” in this article, we mean precisely this **departure from a reference point that can be inferred from the context**.

**The direction of the deviation depends on the word to which ～め attaches**. 多め points toward a greater amount, 少なめ toward a smaller amount; 強め and 弱め indicate greater and lesser intensity, respectively. 

The suffix can therefore accompany variations in opposite directions. Translations such as *“a little more,”* *“a little less,”* or *“slightly”* are often appropriate, but understanding the construction requires one further step: identifying which property is being evaluated and what it is being compared against. It is this comparison that gives concrete meaning to the degree expressed by ～め.

Grammatically, too, adding the suffix changes how the word behaves. With many ～い adjectives, ～め attaches to the stem: 大きい becomes 大きめ and 早い becomes 早め. The resulting forms can modify a noun with ～めの, as in 大きめの鞄, or modify an action with ～めに, as in 早めに来る. The change therefore affects both the nuance of meaning and the way the word functions in the sentence.

In contemporary Japanese, the use of ～め also extends beyond more traditional combinations. Expressions such as きれいめ, used in descriptions of clothing, can be used to characterize a style or tendency. 

In some uses, especially in social-media writing, the suffix can also help make a judgment less categorical: the nuance then concerns not only degree, but also the way the writer chooses to present their evaluation. To interpret it correctly, the base word, the context, and the speaker’s or writer’s communicative intention must therefore be considered together.

In the following sections, we will examine the meaning of the ～め suffix, its formation rules, and the relationship between degree, approximation, and mitigation. We will also look at its origins and modern extensions of use, distinguishing it from the ordinal ～目 found in expressions such as 二人目, “the second person,” in order to understand how the same form can perform different grammatical functions.

## The ～め (me) suffix in Japanese: meaning and usage

The morpheme め (also often written 目), in its “degree” use, functions as a **derivational suffix**: it attaches to a base (typically adjectival, though not exclusively) to create a new form with a different meaning, often changing its grammatical category and expressing that something is **“a little more / a little less”** relative to a contextual standard—in other words, **“tending toward X” without fully coinciding with X**.   

In other words, it indicates a slight variation relative to something else and can often be rendered as **“a little”** or **“slightly.”**


From a grammatical standpoint, many forms in 〜め behave like nouns, adjectival nouns, or [na-adjectives](/en/articles/origin-na-adjectives-japanese-history/): they modify a noun with *〜めの* (and sometimes *〜めな*) and modify a verb with *〜めに*; they can also function as predicates with *だ／です*. 

This suffix is historically related to the use of 目 to form ordinals and is often described as the result of **grammaticalization** (with a branch distinct from the ordinal use of 〜目 meaning “-th”). 
In modern Japanese, in addition to the “classic” forms (*早め*・*多め*・*少なめ*, etc.), the use of 〜め appears to be expanding, especially in media and online, where it can also attach to other suffixes, nouns, adverbs, and verbs.

---


## Origins and historical development

The most plausible historical basis for **degree-marking** 〜め is the noun 目, which is polysemous in Japanese (body parts, “points/marks,” “mesh/joints,” etc.). In some descriptions, it is divided into several subentries and includes uses that are already grammaticalized, such as the formation of expressions of degree (for example, 多め・長め) alongside the traditional ordinal use (for example, 2人目). 


This split is important because the use examined in this article (degree) is not the same as the ordinal use: the latter typically attaches to a **numeral + counter** and produces an ordinal meaning such as *“-th,”* whereas the め *“of degree”* attaches to qualitative or quantitative bases and produces a meaning such as *“a little X.”* 

As for historical attestations, the 〜め lexical items regarded as “standard” are well documented in major historical dictionaries: for example, 早め is glossed as “earlier than normal / expected,” with attestations dating back at least to the seventeenth century; similarly, forms such as 大きめ and 深め are defined as *“comparatively (relatively) large / deep”* and explicitly treated as formations with め as a “接尾語” (suffix).

## Grammatical description and formation rules

From a morphological standpoint, ～め is a **derivational suffix**: it is added to a lexical base to form a new word, changing its meaning and, in many cases, its grammatical behavior. 

The term “derivation” refers precisely to this process of word formation. In 大きい → 大きめ, for example, adding the suffix produces a form that expresses an evaluation of degree and combines with other elements in the sentence differently from the original adjective. **The value of ～め is realized through its connection with the base: in this use, the suffix does not constitute an independent word**.

The most familiar pattern involves **～い adjectives**. In common combinations, ～め attaches to their stem—that is, the part that remains after removing the final い: 大きい → 大きめ, 長い → 長め, 高い → 高め, 早い → 早め. The same process can be seen in 多い → 多め and 少ない → 少なめ. 

The meaning of the base remains recognizable in the derived word: 大きめ concerns size, 長め length, and 多め and 少なめ quantity. The suffix adds a relative evaluation to that property, whose interpretation depends on the reference point established by the context.

The rule *“remove い and add め”* therefore describes many common forms, but it also has exceptions and lexical peculiarities that must be learned on a word-by-word basis. One important case is **濃い → 濃いめ**, in which い is retained. Depending on the context, 濃いめ may refer to a deeper color, a stronger flavor, or a higher concentration. 

The 日本国語大辞典 also notes that, especially in Kansai usage, forms such as 多いめ and 高いめ occur, in which the suffix follows the full adjective. It is therefore necessary to distinguish the most common pattern in standard Japanese from variants that actually occur in usage.

The addition of ～め is also found with **verbal bases**, through the 連用形, or continuative form of the verb. A traditional example is 控える → 控え → 控えめ. The resulting word has developed established uses and can indicate moderation, restraint, or a limited amount, as in 控えめな態度, “a reserved manner,” and 控えめにする, “to keep something within moderate limits.” 

The existence of these examples shows that ～め can also take a verbal base; however, the possibility of forming new words according to this pattern must be assessed on a case-by-case basis.

Studies of contemporary usage document an expansion in the range of bases that can take the suffix. Forms are attested with **～な adjectives** as bases, such as きれい → きれいめ, as well as with adverbs, such as ゆっくり → ゆっくりめ, and nouns, such as ギャル → ギャルめ. In きれいめ, the final い in きれい belongs to the lexical base and is retained: the process is different from that of an ～い adjective such as 大きい. 

These formations demonstrate the productivity of the suffix, but they differ in how widespread they are and in the contexts in which they are used; more creative combinations require particular attention to context and register.

To describe the result of the derivation, the literature often uses the term **nominalizing suffix** (名詞化接尾辞). The NINJAL IPAL manual places ～め among the suffixes that form nouns from adjectival bases. Dictionaries likewise classify various words in ～め, such as 大きめ, 早め, and 少なめ, as nouns or adjectival nouns, depending on how they can be used. 

This terminology concerns the behavior of the resulting form in the sentence: a property originally expressed by an adjective is presented through a new unit capable of entering noun-like constructions.

The change is especially clear when the word modifies a noun. In *大きい鞄*, *“a large bag,”* the adjective 大きい directly precedes 鞄. In the construction *大きめの鞄*, by contrast, the derived form is linked to the noun through の. The difference therefore concerns both the structure and the evaluation being expressed: 大きめ describes a relatively large bag, for example compared with the usual size or with what is expected in the situation. 

The shift from 大きい to 大きめ changes both the way the property is expressed and the way the word connects to the noun.

The construction **～めの + noun** is very common, as shown by 大きめの鞄, 早めの夕食, and 濃いめの化粧. Constructions with な are also found: the dictionary records, for example, 少なめな見積もり, “a somewhat low estimate,” while 控えめな態度 is an established combination. The choice between の and な should therefore be checked against actual usage.

Describing a form as “nominal” or as an “adjectival noun” helps explain how it functions, but the way it links to a noun also depends on the usage patterns of the individual word.

When a form in ～め modifies a verb, **～めに** is normally used. In 早めに出発する, “to leave a little earlier,” 早めに specifies the time of departure relative to a contextual reference point. In 野菜を大きめに切る, “to cut the vegetables into relatively large pieces,” 大きめに instead indicates the desired size of the result. 

**に therefore allows the derived form to function adverbially**. In requests, ～めで is also found, as in ご飯は少なめでお願いします: here, the speaker specifies the desired quantity by asking for a smaller portion of rice.

Forms in ～め can also appear as **predicates with だ or です**: この鞄は大きめです means that the bag is relatively large compared with the relevant reference point. 

To express the past, one can say 大きめだった or 大きめでした. The comparison with 大きい → 大きかった makes the grammatical shift visible: after derivation, the word behaves like a nominal form, and tense information is expressed through the copula. 

Using ～め correctly therefore requires considering two connected steps: how the word is formed from its base and the construction into which that new form is inserted.

## Degree and approximation

In expressions of degree, ～め makes it possible to present a property in terms of a graded assessment: a relatively large size, a smaller quantity, a more pronounced intensity, or a time earlier than the relevant point of reference. 

To understand its meaning, three factors need to be considered together: **the property expressed by the base, the reference point against which it is evaluated, and the extent of the difference suggested by the context**.

Dictionary definitions show that this relationship takes different forms in individual words. The *Digital Daijisen* describes 大きめ in terms of something perceived as relatively large; it describes 多め and 少なめ as indicating, respectively, a somewhat greater or smaller quantity; for 早め, it indicates a time earlier than a set time or period. 

Translations such as *“a little bigger”*, *“a little less”*, and *“a little earlier”* are therefore often appropriate, provided that the comparison underlying them is properly identified.

### The reference point

The **reference standard** may be a concrete measurement, a customary norm, an expectation, or what is considered appropriate in a given situation. In 大きめの鞄, for example, the size may be evaluated in relation to other bags of the same type, the size the speaker would normally choose, or the amount of space needed for a particular use. 

The context makes it possible to identify the relevant point of reference; the suffix itself does not specify what that reference consists of.

This reference can also be expressed explicitly in the sentence. In いつもより早めに出発する, *“to leave a little earlier than usual”*, いつもより explicitly establishes the usual departure time as the point of comparison. Similarly, in 予想より多めの応募者, *“a slightly higher number of applicants than expected”*, the quantity is compared with a forecast. The use of ～め is therefore fully compatible with an explicitly stated point of comparison.

When the reference remains implicit, it is reconstructed from the communicative situation. A request such as ご飯は少なめでお願いします, *“a slightly smaller portion of rice, please”*, is understood in relation to the portion normally served or the amount the other person would otherwise have prepared. The sentence guides the choice of quantity without specifying an exact weight.

*“Standard”* should therefore be understood as **the reference point relevant to the assessment being made**. It does not necessarily correspond to a statistical average, a universal measurement, or the value regarded as best. A customary portion, an expected time, and a size that suits someone’s body are different kinds of reference point, even when all of them are described using forms in ～め.

### Direction and extent of the variation

The lexical base determines the direction of the assessment. 多め points toward a greater quantity, while 少なめ points toward a smaller one; 高め toward higher values and 低め toward lower ones; 強め toward greater intensity and 弱め toward reduced intensity. The contribution of the suffix remains identifiable in both directions: what is presented is a degree that tends toward the property expressed by the base.

For this reason, **an increase in the degree of a property does not always correspond to an increase in the actual quantity involved**. In 少なめ, the quantity is presented as more limited and is therefore lower than the reference point; in 弱め, weakness is more pronounced, and the intensity is reduced. 

In translation, it is necessary to follow the meaning of the derived word, choosing *“more”*, *“less”*, *“earlier”*, *“weaker”*, or another appropriate formulation.

Many forms in ～め normally suggest a relatively limited difference. This reading accounts for the frequent use of translations such as *“a little”*, *“slightly”*, or *“rather”*. The actual extent of the difference, however, depends on the property and the situation: what counts as *“a little early”* when planning a trip may involve a very different amount of time from what counts as *“a little early”* for a short meeting. The suffix does not specify a fixed percentage, duration, or distance.

Adverbs also help to specify the degree more precisely. In 少し大きめ, 少し presents the difference as small; a combination such as かなり多め, by contrast, can indicate a quantity noticeably greater than the reference point. The latter is also attested in institutional texts. The fact that ～め can occur with such modifiers shows that **the common moderate reading does not amount to a rigid numerical limit**.

Its distribution may also be influenced by conventions within a particular field. In its meteorological terminology, the Japan Meteorological Agency assigns 高め・低め and 多め・少なめ the same meaning as the corresponding expressions 高い・低い and 多い・少ない. 

This is a convention specific to that field, but it is useful in showing why a single translation such as *“slightly”* cannot be applied indiscriminately to every occurrence.

### Approximation and the relationship with the base adjective

Within this framework, we can speak of **approximation in the description of degree**: the expression characterizes a size, quantity, or intensity without fully quantifying it. Describing a bag as 大きめ places it on the relatively large side of the scale, while leaving the context to determine the relevant size and the extent of the difference from the reference point.

This kind of approximation should be distinguished from a numerical estimate. *“About three hundred grams”* proposes a value around which the actual quantity may vary; 少なめ, by contrast, presents a quantity as reduced relative to the relevant reference point. These two ways of describing quantity serve different purposes and can even occur together: knowing the exact weight of a portion does not prevent someone from judging it to be 少なめ.

It is equally important to distinguish the precision of the measurement from the speaker’s certainty in making the judgment. A speaker may know exactly how large an object is and still choose a form in ～め because they want to describe it through a practical comparison. **The absence of explicit quantification in the word does not necessarily imply uncertainty about the situation being described**.

The base adjective itself also often requires a context-dependent assessment. To interpret 大きい, “large,” we need to know which dimensions are relevant in the situation: a bag that is large for everyday use may be small compared with bags used for a long trip. The distinction between 大きい and 大きめ therefore does not amount to a rigid opposition between absolute and relative size.

The definition of 大きめ as *“relatively large”* also cautions against assuming an automatic ranking in which 大きめ would always be taken to mean “not as large as” 大きい. The same bag may be described using either form, depending on the reference point and on how the speaker chooses to frame the assessment. 

In some contexts, ～め makes the judgment more restrained; in others, it highlights a size that is greater than usual or greater than what is considered suitable for the purpose in question.

The addition of the suffix therefore does not, by itself, allow us to conclude that the object lacks the property expressed by the adjective. Formulations such as *“on the larger side”* can help convey the direction of the assessment, provided that they are not interpreted as a rule according to which the object could never be genuinely large. 

The relationship with the base must be reconstructed from the word’s actual use.

### Describing a degree or indicating an adjustment

**Forms in ～め can describe a characteristic that is already present or indicate the degree one wishes to achieve**. 大きめの鞄 presents the size of a bag; 野菜を大きめに切る, *“to cut the vegetables into relatively large pieces”*, instead specifies the desired outcome of the action. 

In the latter case, the reference point may be the usual way of cutting the vegetables or the size normally expected for the preparation in question.

The same relationship appears in 濃いめに味つけをする, *“to season something so that the flavor is a little stronger”*. The form suggests an adjustment relative to the flavor taken as the reference point, while leaving some room in how that adjustment is carried out. How strong the result should be depends on the dish, personal preferences, and the shared expectations of the people involved.

The direction of the adjustment does not by itself determine whether the result is desirable. Choosing 多め may be deliberate, for example to allow for some extra, while 少なめ may simply reflect the portion someone prefers. Any judgment as to whether the amount is excessive, insufficient, or appropriate depends on the context and on the other elements in the sentence.

When interpreting ～め, it is therefore useful to reconstruct three distinct elements: **which property is being graded, which reference point guides the comparison, and which words or circumstances indicate how great the difference is**. This makes it possible to choose an appropriate translation and to recognize when “a little” successfully captures the nuance and when a different formulation is required.

</p>

<p>
With adjectives, this standard is immediately apparent (“larger than normal”),

</p>

<p>
The crucial point is that **〜め does not itself encode a fixed numerical or absolute value**, but rather **a relative adjustment with respect to a standard that may be contextual and is often subjective**.
</p>

## Pragmatic functions: mitigation and “diplomacy” 

Recent studies note that, in addition to its traditional degree meaning, in digital and conversational contexts 〜め can be used to **avoid wording something too directly**: for example, rather than expressing a categorical judgment, a “-め” form may be used as a mitigation strategy (配慮表現), even with evaluative bases that were not traditionally associated with the suffix.


  From this perspective, saying “Xめ” does not merely mean “slightly X,” but can also communicate “I’m putting it this way with some reservation / without pinning the object down to an absolute label,” functioning as a **buffer** especially in settings where a judgment might come across as aggressive or overly assertive.

*<p class="jp-example">ご飯は少なめでお願いします。</p>*  *“I’d like a smaller portion of rice, please.” Note: A typical service/request formula: 少なめ = “a somewhat reduced amount.”*

*<p class="jp-example">砂糖は多めにしてもらえますか。</p>*  *“Could you add a little more (sugar)?”  Note: 多め means “more than normal” without specifying an amount; with に, it helps specify the requested amount.* 


*<p class="jp-example">この靴、少し大きめですね。</p>*  *“These shoes are a little on the large side (larger than expected).”  Note: Size is treated as a scale; “-め” signals a moderate deviation.*  
*<p class="jp-example">大きめのバッグが欲しいです。</p>*  *“I’d like a slightly larger bag.”  Note: Canonical 〜めのN pattern, with nominal/形容動詞-like behavior.*  

*<p class="jp-example">味は薄めがいいです。</p>*  *“I prefer a slightly lighter flavor (less intense).”  Note: “-め” can also point toward “less,” depending on the base (薄い).*

*<p class="jp-example">(SNS/colloquial）今日は大人めで行きたい。</p>*  *“Today I want to go for a slightly more ‘grown-up’ look.”  Note: Extension to nominal/evaluative bases; documented as a modern usage.* 


*<p class="jp-example">（SNS/colloquial）基本、黒多めで。</p>*   *“Basically, keep it mostly black.” Note: A typical example of “style adjustment” (proportion) cited in analyses of contemporary texts.*

## Conclusions

Understanding the ～め suffix means recognizing the relationship between a property and the reference point against which it is evaluated. In its degree use, the base word determines the direction of the variation, while the context tells us what is being treated as normal, usual, or expected. 

The English translation may vary, but the interpretive principle remains the same: identify the quality being described, the point of comparison, and the nuance the suffix adds to the expression.

For learners of Japanese, one useful habit is to pair the translation with a question: *“Compared with what?”*. When encountering a form with ～め, it is worth looking to the context for the reference point that makes the evaluation understandable: an expected time, a usual quantity, a personal preference. 

Noting this element alongside the sentence helps you remember when the expression is appropriate and recognize its meaning even when a translation such as “a little more” or “slightly” leaves part of the information implicit.

**When producing Japanese, it is useful to start with established combinations and learn them together with the words and particles that accompany them**. One useful exercise is to compare authentic formulations with and without ～め, observing both the grammatical construction and the effect on the description or request. 

You can then try to explain in your own words which reference point is being left implicit and what changes in the evaluation. This comparison helps you use the suffix more deliberately while taking the peculiarities of individual forms into account.

Modern extensions, especially those encountered on social media, also require attention to register. To internalize them, it is useful to observe multiple occurrences and check who uses them, in which settings, and with what intention. 

When ～め helps soften a judgment, the tone emerges from the sentence as a whole and from the communicative situation: it may convey caution, tact, or even irony. Recognizing a formation and being able to use it naturally are therefore skills that develop through exposure to different contexts.

The goal is to learn to connect form, meaning, and context of use. With this awareness, ～め becomes a resource for expressing preferences, requests, and evaluations more precisely, deciding case by case how explicitly to state the comparison and how to present one’s judgment.
