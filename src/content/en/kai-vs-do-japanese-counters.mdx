In Japanese, what is the difference between 回 (kai) and 度 (do) when counting how many times something happens?




回 (かい, kai) and 度 (ど, do) can both indicate how many times something happens or is done. 

- 回 is especially well suited to counting repetitions; 
- 度 more readily highlights the occasions on which an event occurs. 



When 回 and 度 are used to say how many times something happens, the difference is not simply numerical. Both can be translated as “time,” and in many cases they can refer to the same event. 

What changes slightly is how the event is viewed. **回 tends to present the different occurrences (times) as units in a series to be counted**: the focus falls primarily on the number of repetitions. 

**度, by contrast, more readily preserves the idea of each individual “time” as an occasion on which something happens**. 

In other words, 回 more naturally focuses on **how many times** an event is repeated, whereas 度 can bring **the occasions on which** that event occurred more clearly into focus.

Put simply, **回 tends to focus primarily on the number, whereas 度 more readily presents the individual occurrences as occasions on which something happens**. The difference is not absolute, because both can count how many times an event occurs, and in many contexts they are interchangeable. 

However, **回 more naturally presents events as units in a series**: what matters most is how many repetitions there have been. **度, on the other hand, more clearly retains its original sense of “time,” “occasion,” or “point at which something happens”**; for this reason, it can draw attention not only to the total, but also to the fact that the event occurred on particular occasions. 

This is a subtle distinction rather than a rigid rule, but it provides the most useful starting point for understanding why the two counters, despite often overlapping, are not always perceived in exactly the same way.

This nuance is also consistent with dictionary definitions: 回数 properly denotes the number of times something is repeated or occurs, while 度（たび） can refer both to the number of times and to the occasion on which an action or situation occurs. As we have seen, there are differences in nuance, construction, and usage.

The difficulty lies precisely in this broad overlap. If both can correspond to the English word “time,” simply looking for two different translations is not enough to distinguish them. We need to look at which aspect of the event is brought to the foreground: its place within a count, its recurrence, or the occasion as considered in context.


A useful way to approach the distinction is to separate three levels: 

- The basic meaning of the two counters; 
- The preferences that emerge in different situations; 
- The expressions that have developed a function of their own. 


A usage tendency is not the same thing as a grammatical requirement, and the fact that an expression is built with 度 does not mean that 度 can automatically be replaced by 回.


The following table compares the different uses of 回 and 度, distinguishing differences in nuance and usage tendencies from actual grammatical constraints.

In the paragraphs that follow, we will examine these points in greater detail through explanations and examples, looking at when the two counters can be used interchangeably, when one sounds more natural than the other, and when the meaning of the construction as a whole needs to be taken into account.

## Counting occurrences in Japanese: 回 (kai) and 度 (do)

回 and 度 belong to the category of 助数詞 (じょすうし, josūshi), counters that combine with numerals. In this case, what is being counted is the occurrence of an action or event: the basic structure is therefore number + 回 or number + 度.

The reading changes depending on the counter. 一回 is read いっかい (ikkai), with consonant gemination, whereas 一度 is read いちど (ichido). Even when the meanings coincide, the two forms therefore retain their own pronunciation.

The scope of the comparison also needs to be defined. The kanji 度 also appears in measurements of temperature and angle, as well as in words relating to degree or intensity. These uses do not involve counting “times” and follow different combinatorial patterns. The presence of the same character does not mean that all of its uses are identical.

---


## 回: counting repetitions as units in a series

**With 回, the different instances of an event can readily be treated as units in a count**. The speaker can indicate their quantity, specify the number of repetitions, or place them within a sequence.

It is this suitability for counting that makes **回 particularly appropriate in instructions, numerical record-keeping, and activities organized into multiple sessions**.
The notion of a “series” does not require the events to be perfectly identical. 

Two instances may differ in duration or outcome and still count as two units within the same tally. What matters is that they are treated as occurrences of the same type of activity.
This is why 回 is frequently associated with events that are expected to be repeated. 

An activity scheduled over several sessions naturally lends itself to numbering. However, the fact that it can be counted does not mean that it must continue: 回 does not, by itself, state that there will be another time afterward.

Even 一回 can indicate a single performance, with no expectation that it will be repeated. Any such limit is specified by the context and by elements such as だけ, “only.” The choice of 回 concerns how the event is presented; it is not a prediction about its future.

---


## 度: viewing the occasions on which something happens

度 can express the same number of occurrences as 回, but **it is often found when those occurrences are presented as episodes, experiences, or occasions considered individually**. 

These occurrences do not necessarily have to fit into a program or predetermined sequence.
For this reason, 度 is often associated with events whose repetition is not guaranteed or taken for granted. The speaker looks at the occasions on which something happened without necessarily treating them as stages in an activity expected to continue.

One useful way of understanding this tendency is that 度 more readily allows the occasion itself to become salient in the discourse. The context may bring out the value of an experience, surprise at its recurrence, or the wish that it not happen again. These nuances, however, depend on the sentence as a whole: 度 does not in itself mean “exceptional,” “emotional,” or “undesirable.”

It would therefore be misleading to contrast a supposedly always-neutral 回 with an always-emotional 度. Both can simply communicate a number. The difference is most noticeable when the context gives prominence to how the speaker regards those occurrences.

---


## 回 and 度 with frequency: regularity does not decide everything

The distinction is sometimes reduced to the formula *“回 for regular events, 度 for irregular ones.”* This can serve as an initial guideline, but **it does not account for all actual usage**.

**回 is a particularly natural choice when frequency is being recorded as numerical data**. However, **度 can also appear with a time interval and refer to a periodic event**. 

The structure “time interval + に + 一度” is completely normal: **the presence of a predictable schedule does not make 回 obligatory**.

Research by Keiko Kuriyama based on several corpora shows precisely that **度 also occurs with regular events spaced apart over time**. In the material examined, forms with 度 were frequent when the intervals were long. This finding suggests that we should distinguish the periodicity of the event from the way each recurrence is presented.

The length of the interval should not, however, be turned into a new rule. **There is no time threshold beyond which one must switch from 回 to 度**. What also matters is whether the discourse emphasizes the rhythm of the repetitions or the occasion that comes around again.


---


## 一回 and 一度: the different meanings of “once”

### When they simply indicate a single occurrence

In their most straightforward use, 一回 and 一度 can both mean “once.” If the context is asking only how many times something occurred, the difference may be minimal and may not require a different translation.

The same applies to もう一回 and もう一度, both of which can mean “one more time” or “again.” **The presence of もう explicitly adds another occurrence**: 度, too, is therefore fully compatible with repetition and is quite commonly used in this way.

### When 一度 presents something as an opportunity to try

In invitations, suggestions, and expressions of desire, **一度 can present an action as something worth trying or as an opportunity to experience something firsthand**. The numeral one recedes into the background, while the possibility of having the experience comes to the fore.

In these contexts, interpreting it as “exactly once, and no more” can impose a limit that the speaker does not intend. **Depending on the context, the meaning can approach “sometime” or “at least once.”**

This possibility does not make 一回 incompatible with invitations or with the idea of trying something: the dictionary 大辞泉 also records this type of use. The difference needs to be assessed in the expression as a whole, without assigning each form an exclusive communicative intention.

### When 一度 means “once” in the sense of “once you…”
一度 also has an adverbial use that can come close to **“once…”**, **“now that…”**, or **“if ever,”** especially in conditional constructions. The focus shifts away from the number of events and toward the occurrence of a condition and what follows from it.

This use is similar to that of another adverb, 一旦 (ittan), which we will return to in a future article. 

一度 (一旦) can appear on its own, or it can reinforce a sentence that already contains a conjunction such as 以上.

For example:

*<p class="jp-example">一度始めた以上、最後までやり遂げなければならない。</p>*
*“Once you have started, you have to see it through to the end.”*

Here 一度 has precisely the sense of *“once…”*: it presents the action as a point beyond which the situation changes. It works in tandem with 以上 and reinforces the idea: *“now that you have started / given that you have taken this step, a consequence or obligation follows.”*
The combination 一度～以上 is therefore especially effective when *“once…”* implies that one cannot simply return to the previous situation.

It is worth noting that the sentence would still be grammatical and meaningful without 一度 (一旦). In this use, 一度 is not required by the 以上 construction, but reinforces the temporal sense of “once / once the action has begun.”

Thus, in this use, the relevant question is not only “how many times?” but also “what happens once this situation comes about?” This is one of the reasons why 一度 cannot be analyzed in every sentence as a simple combination of numeral and counter.

The translation must therefore follow the relationship between the parts of the sentence. Rendering 一度 systematically as “once” only in a numerical sense can obscure precisely the temporal or conditional relationship that organizes the discourse.

## 何回 and 何度: asking “how many times” and emphasizing repetition

何回 (なんかい, nankai) and 何度 (なんど, nando) can both ask how many times something has happened. 何度 does not presuppose that the number is impossible to know, just as 何回 does not require the events to have been planned.

**With も, the forms 何回も and 何度も can indicate numerous repetitions: “many times,” “over and over.”** In these uses, the precise number can remain in the background with both counters.

何度も can lend itself particularly well to emphasizing the recurrence of an experience. However, 何回も can also express impatience, exasperation, or perseverance. The counter contributes to the nuance, while the overall interpretation depends on the predicate, the other expressions used and, in speech, intonation.

It is also necessary to distinguish the number of repetitions from the period over which they occur. Neither 何回 nor 何度 determines on its own whether we are talking about closely spaced attempts, episodes spread over years, or a recurring experience.

## Grammatical limits: numbers, series, and ordinals

### Can 度 be used only up to three?
No. 一度, 二度, and 三度 are very common forms, but there is no general prohibition against using 度 with numbers greater than three. Various teaching materials include examples of 度 used with higher numbers.

This does not mean that 回 and 度 are equally natural with every quantity and in every context. **For ordinary counting, especially as the number grows larger, 回 is generally the less marked choice**. 

**With 度, the narrative context, register, or expressive intention can play a greater role**.


A preference for smaller numbers therefore reflects a tendency in usage. It does not justify treating every form above 三度 as automatically incorrect.


### Zero and decimal numbers

A clearer restriction concerns counting with zero and decimal values. When indicating the number of occurrences, 回 is used: **0回 can record the absence of any events, while a decimal number followed by 回 can express a statistical average**.


In the latter case, a single action is not necessarily being divided into fractions: it is the average value, calculated across multiple periods or observations, that is not a whole number. 度 is not normally used in the same way as a counter for occurrences.
This restriction does not apply to 度 when it is used as a unit of temperature or angle. In those meanings, zero and decimal values are perfectly possible: what changes is the function of the word, not the written form of the character.


### 第～回, ～回目 and ～度目

To number editions or parts of a series formally, the construction 第 + number + 回 is used. 度 does not replace 回 in this structure. Likewise, 全 + number + 回, which presents the total number of parts in a series, is another characteristic use of 回.

It does not follow from this that 度 is incompatible with ordinals. The suffix 目 (め, me) can be added to both: 二回目 and 二度目 can indicate the second time, just as 三回目 and 三度目 can indicate the third.

The two constructions should therefore be kept distinct. 

- 第～回 identifies an item through the numbering of a series; 
- ～回目 and ～度目 place an occurrence in relation to the ones that came before it. 

With 目, the choice of counter can retain the nuances associated with counting or with the salience of the occasion.

---

## 一度も and 一回も in negative sentences

**The constructions 一度も～ない and 一回も～ない both express the absence of even a single occurrence**: “not even once,” and therefore, depending on the context, “never.”

The pattern is based on the minimum unit indicated by 一 and on negation, reinforced by も. There is no need to resort to the numerical value zero: what is denied is that even a single occurrence has taken place. This is why the fact that 0度 is not normally used in counting does not prevent 一度も from expressing the complete absence of an experience.

- 一度も is common when talking about something one has never experienced or done; 
- 一回も remains equally possible when negating a count. 

The mere presence of negation therefore does not force the choice of one counter over the other.

---


## When 度 is part of an expression with its own meaning


### 一度に: doing several things at once

一度に (いちどに, ichido ni) is an adverbial expression that can mean “all at once,” “simultaneously,” or “in one go.” Its core meaning is not necessarily the number of repetitions, but the concentration of several elements or actions within the same occasion.

For this reason, 一度に cannot be reduced to the generic formula “once” followed by the particle に. In its sense of simultaneity or doing something all at once, it should be recognized as an expression with its own established use. Mechanically replacing 度 with 回 does not guarantee that the same meaning will be preserved.

### 二度と～ない: ruling out something happening again

In the construction 二度と～ない (nido to… nai), the meaning is “never… again.” 二度と combines with negation to rule out any further occurrence, and it cannot be freely replaced with 二回と.
The numeral two does not imply that the event previously happened exactly once. 

The expression can also be used after numerous repetitions: what is being ruled out is that it will happen again.
**The difference from 一度も～ない is therefore temporal and semantic**. 

- 一度も～ない denies that the event occurs even once within the relevant span; 
- 二度と～ない rules out its recurrence from a particular point onward.

## Comparative examples with 回 and 度

### Counting repetitions of an action

*<p class="jp-example">このボタンを三回押してください。</p>*
*“Press this button three times.”*

*三回 specifies the required number of presses. 回 is particularly natural in instructions that tell someone how many times to perform an action.*

*<p class="jp-example">このボタンを三度押してください。</p>*
*“Press this button three times.”*

*三度 can also indicate three presses. The practical meaning remains the same, although 三回 is the more usual choice in an instruction of this kind.*

### Referring to an experience that happened only once

*<p class="jp-example">その作家には一回だけ会ったことがあります。</p>*
*“I’ve only met that writer once.”*

*一回 presents the meeting in numerical terms: a single occurrence. The sentence does not necessarily suggest that there will be another meeting.*

*<p class="jp-example">その作家には一度だけ会ったことがあります。</p>*
*“I have met that writer only once.”*

*一度 fits naturally when recounting a past occasion or experience. Here it does not require a different translation from 一回: in both sentences, だけ makes it explicit that the meeting occurred only once.*

### Indicating a regular frequency

*<p class="jp-example">月に一回、家族で外食します。</p>*
*“I eat out with my family once a month.”*

*月に一回 expresses the frequency of the activity: one meal out per month. 回 allows it to be presented as numerical data.*

*<p class="jp-example">月に一度、家族で外食します。</p>*
*“I eat out with my family once a month.”*

*月に一度 is equally natural and indicates the same monthly frequency. 度 can therefore also refer to an occasion that recurs regularly.*

## Examples of other uses

### もう一回 and もう一度: asking for a repetition

*<p class="jp-example">もう一回、最初から読んでください。</p>*
*“Please read it again from the beginning.”*

*もう一回 asks for the action to be performed one more time. もう adds another occurrence to those that have already taken place.*

*<p class="jp-example">もう一度、名前を教えていただけますか。</p>*
*“Could you tell me your name again?”*

*もう一度 asks for the information to be given again. The politeness comes mainly from ～ていただけますか, not from the use of 度 by itself.*

### 一度 and 一回: inviting someone to have an experience

*<p class="jp-example">よかったら、一度うちに遊びに来てください。</p>*
*“If you like, come over sometime.”*

*一度 presents the visit as an opportunity worth taking. The invitation does not establish that the person may come only once.*

*<p class="jp-example">一回、この方法でやってみませんか。</p>*
*“Why don’t we try doing it this way?”*

*一回 can also be used when inviting the listener to try something. Here what matters is the invitation to try; the sentence does not rule out repeating the experience.*

### 一度: “once…”

*<p class="jp-example">一度このゲームを始めると、なかなかやめられません。</p>*
*“Once you start this game, it is hard to stop.”*

*一度 accompanies the condition expressed by ～と: once you start playing, it becomes difficult to stop. The focus is on the condition being met and on its consequence.*

### 何回 and 何度: asking “how many times”

*<p class="jp-example">先月は何回図書館に行きましたか。</p>*
*“How many times did you go to the library last month?”*

*何回 asks for the number of visits within a specific period. The answer can give an exact number.*

*<p class="jp-example">これまでに何度引っ越しましたか。</p>*
*“How many times have you moved house so far?”*

*何度 asks how many times an event has occurred over the course of one’s life. Here too, it is entirely possible to answer with an exact number.*

### 何回も and 何度も: emphasizing repetition

*<p class="jp-example">その話は何回も聞きましたから、もう説明しなくても大丈夫です。</p>*
*“I’ve heard that story many times, so you don’t need to explain it to me again.”*

*何回も indicates numerous repetitions without specifying the number. Depending on the context and intonation, it can also convey a certain degree of impatience.*

*<p class="jp-example">何度も読み返して、ようやく意味が分かりました。</p>*
*“I reread it over and over and finally understood what it meant.”*

*何度も highlights the repeated act of rereading. The sense of perseverance emerges from the sentence as a whole, especially from ようやく (“finally”), which highlights the eventual result.*

### 度 with numbers greater than three

*<p class="jp-example">彼女はこの大会で五度優勝しています。</p>*
*“She has won this competition five times.”*

*五度 is natural when reporting sports victories. 度 can accompany numbers greater than three: there is no grammatical limit at 三度.*

### 0回: recording the absence of occurrences

*<p class="jp-example">今月の欠席回数は0回でした。</p>*
*“The number of absences this month was zero.”*

*0回 records a count of zero. 回 is used to express zero occurrences; 0度 has other uses, such as measuring temperature.*

### 回 with a decimal average

*<p class="jp-example">この一か月は、週に平均2.5回ジムに通いました。</p>*
*“Over the past month, I went to the gym an average of 2.5 times a week.”*

*2.5回 expresses an average weekly frequency. It does not mean that someone made “half a visit”: the decimal value results from calculating the average.*

### 第～回: numbering editions

*<p class="jp-example">来月、第十回日本語スピーチコンテストが開かれます。</p>*
*“The tenth Japanese-language speech contest will be held next month.”*

*第十回 identifies the event as its tenth edition. In the construction 第 + number + 回, 度 does not replace 回.*

### 全～回: indicating the total number of sessions

*<p class="jp-example">この講座は全六回です。</p>*
*“This course consists of six sessions in total.”*

*全六回 presents the total number of sessions that make up the course. 全 indicates the series as a whole.*

### ～回目 and ～度目: placing an occurrence within a sequence

*<p class="jp-example">この店に来るのは、今日で三回目です。</p>*
*“Today is the third time I’ve come to this shop.”*

*三回目 places the current visit after the previous two. The suffix 目 turns the count into an ordinal expression.*

*<p class="jp-example">三度目の挑戦で、ようやく試験に合格しました。</p>*
*“I finally passed the exam on my third attempt.”*

*三度目 correctly indicates the third attempt. 度 can also be followed by 目: the restriction that applies to the construction 第～回 does not extend to these ordinals.*

### 一回も and 一度も in negative sentences

*<p class="jp-example">今日は一回も外に出ていません。</p>*
*“I haven’t gone outside even once today.”*

*一回も, together with negation, rules out even a single instance of going outside. The period under consideration is limited to today.*

*<p class="jp-example">これまで一度も海外に行ったことがありません。</p>*
*“I have never been abroad.”*

*一度も～たことがない denies that the experience has ever occurred up to the present moment. 一度も can therefore express the complete absence of an experience.*

### 一度に: doing several things at the same time

*<p class="jp-example">みんなが一度に話すと、何も聞き取れません。</p>*
*“If everyone talks at once, I can’t make out what anyone is saying.”*

*一度に indicates that the voices overlap. Its meaning is “simultaneously,” rather than simply counting a single occurrence.*

### 二度と～ない: “never… again”

*<p class="jp-example">これまで何度も遅刻しましたが、二度と遅刻しません。</p>*
*“I’ve been late many times before, but I’ll never be late again.”*

*二度と, with negation, rules out any further lateness. Here it is used after numerous previous occurrences: it does not presuppose that the action happened only once.*

### 度 as a unit of temperature and angle

*<p class="jp-example">今朝の気温は0度でした。</p>*
*“The temperature this morning was zero degrees.”*

*Here 度 measures temperature. 0度 is correct because we are not counting how many times an event occurred.*

*<p class="jp-example">この二つの角度の差は0.5度です。</p>*
*“The difference between these two angles is 0.5 degrees.”*

*度 indicates an angular measurement and can take a decimal value. This use is distinct from 度 as a counter for occurrences.*

## Conclusions

We have seen that the regularity of an event, the number of repetitions, and the speaker’s involvement do not by themselves determine the choice between 回 and 度. 

- 度 can accompany periodic events and numbers greater than three; 
- 回 can also count a single occurrence. 

The uses examined therefore reveal broad overlap in ordinary counting, with preferences depending on the context and on how the events are presented.

**The clearest constraints emerge in grammatical constructions.** When counting occurrences, zero, decimal averages, and structures such as 第～回 and 全～回 require 回, whereas ordinals with 目 allow both 回 and 度. 

Questions and negative sentences also show considerable overlap: 何回 and 何度 can both be used to ask how many times something happened, while 一回も～ない and 一度も～ない can both deny even a single occurrence.

Some expressions, however, go beyond this basic counting function. 

- 一度 can take on the meaning of “once…”; 
- 一度に concentrates several actions or elements into the same occasion; 
- 二度と～ない rules out something happening again. 

In these cases, recognizing the construction as a whole allows us to distinguish meanings that the English word “time” alone cannot capture.

For learners, the practical advice is therefore to **use 回 as a starting point for straightforward counting and learn expressions with their own established meanings as complete units**. To refine your choice in contexts that allow both, it is useful to collect authentic sentences along with the contexts in which they occur. A less common formulation is not necessarily wrong: before correcting it, we need to determine whether we are dealing with a genuine grammatical constraint or simply a usage preference.

In other words, when forming a sentence, the first thing to check is whether you are simply counting occurrences or using an expression with a specific meaning. 

**For straightforward counting, 回 is often a natural starting point**, especially in instructions and numerical data. If, on the other hand, you encounter 一度に, 二度と～ない, or 一度 with the meaning “once…,” you need to consider how the entire construction works: **the English translation “time” by itself is not enough to guide the choice**.

A good study method might be to note down a small number of complete authentic sentences while preserving the context in which they appear. Beside each one, record the speaker’s purpose: to communicate a number, recount an experience, suggest an activity, or rule out something happening again. 

It is also worth observing the verb and particles that accompany the counter, because the nuance arises from the combination as a whole. 

Memorize fixed expressions as complete units and, when a use seems surprising, look for similar examples in a dictionary: this makes it possible to determine whether you are dealing with a particular construction or simply one of the options allowed by the context.

When correcting exercises, grammatical errors should be kept separate from formulations that are merely less common. When a sentence allows both 回 and 度, there is no need to assume that every substitution carries a major difference in meaning, nor to assume that one of the two versions is wrong. **Confidence comes from recognizing the constraints of the language; naturalness improves by observing the choices speakers make in real situations.**
