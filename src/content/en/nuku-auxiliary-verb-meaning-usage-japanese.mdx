**What does ～抜く (ぬく, nuku) mean, how do you use it in Japanese compound verbs, and what does it add to the base verb's meaning?**



In Japanese, **～抜く (～ぬく, nuku)** is a verb that, when used as an auxiliary and attached to another verb, forms **compound verbs expressing the completion of an action, perseverance in carrying it through, or the attainment of an especially high degree of intensity**. 

Its meaning, however, goes beyond simply *“finishing”* or *“completing”* something: ～抜く often conveys a determination to **see something through, face difficulties, overcome obstacles, or remain resolute despite the effort involved**. 

In other contexts, it can instead emphasize how thoroughly an action has been carried out or how intensely a particular state has been experienced.

To understand these nuances, it helps to begin with the original meaning of **抜く (ぬく)** as an independent verb. Its primary meanings are *“to extract,” “to pull out,” “to draw out,” or “to remove”* something, such as a nail driven into wood or a splinter lodged in the skin. 

The kanji **抜**, which contains the radical 扌 (hand), evokes precisely this idea of physically pulling something out. Alongside this core meaning, 抜く also has senses associated with passing through, overcoming a barrier, or crossing a boundary. A common image thus emerges: **moving past resistance or an obstacle to reach a point beyond it**.

This image helps explain how ～抜く developed its auxiliary meaning. When it combines with another verb, the obstacle being overcome need no longer be physical: it may instead take the form of exertion, difficulties encountered during an activity, limits to be surpassed, or the determination needed to achieve a goal. 

The action expressed by the main verb is therefore presented not merely in terms of what happens, but also in terms of **its continuity, intensity, or being carried through to the end**.

This combination gives rise to verbs such as **やり抜く (やりぬく)**, “to see something through without giving up”; **考え抜く (かんがえぬく)**, “to think something through thoroughly, examining every aspect of an issue”; **生き抜く (いきぬく)**, “to survive, to keep going despite adversity”; **守り抜く (まもりぬく)**, “to defend or protect something to the very end”; and **耐え抜く (たえぬく)**, “to endure, to withstand a difficult situation until the end.” 

In each of these compounds, ～抜く adds a perspective to the first verb that may emphasize perseverance, the overcoming of difficulties, or the thoroughness with which an action is carried out.

The difference from the base verbs (as we will explore later) is particularly significant. **考える** simply means “to think” or “to reflect,” whereas **考え抜く** suggests thinking something through thoroughly, examining an issue from every angle.

Similarly, **やる** refers to performing an action, whereas **やり抜く** emphasizes seeing it through, often despite obstacles along the way. The contrast between **生きる**, “to live,” and **生き抜く**, “to survive adversity,” likewise shows how adding ～抜く can substantially change the way an action is portrayed while preserving the base verb’s essential meaning.

Not all compound verbs ending in ～抜く, however, necessarily express perseverance or completion. In some combinations, such as **苦しみ抜く (くるしみぬく)**, “to suffer intensely,” the final element primarily conveys an extreme degree of intensity. 

There are also lexicalized compounds, such as **引き抜く (ひきぬく)**, “to pull out,” and **見抜く (みぬく)**, “to see through appearances, to discern,” in which the meaning of 抜く develops along different semantic lines. Understanding these distinctions is important if we are to avoid treating every verb ending in 抜く as merely an intensified form of its base verb.

In the sections that follow, we will explore the **meaning and usage of ～抜く in Japanese grammar**, before examining its **formation and grammatical structure**. 

We will then analyze the **differences in meaning between base verbs and their corresponding ～抜く compounds**, focusing on the nuances introduced by the auxiliary and the restrictions governing which verbs it can combine with. Next, we will consider **register and usage contexts** to understand when and why these compounds are preferred over their simple counterparts. 

Finally, through a series of annotated usage examples and a concluding discussion, we will bring together the different aspects of how ～抜く works and establish practical guidelines for using it correctly.

## ～抜く (ぬく, nuku): Meaning and Usage as a Standalone Verb and an Auxiliary


The verb **抜く (ぬく, nuku)** has a range of meanings that, although they develop in different directions, remain connected to a few fundamental ideas: **extracting, removing, passing through, or going beyond something**. Depending on the context, these actions may involve physical objects, obstacles, limits, or more abstract situations. 

When 抜く appears as the second element of a compound verb, however, its meaning may shift toward a grammatical function that highlights **the completion, perseverance, or intensity of the action expressed by the main verb**.

To understand how these meanings develop, we need to distinguish between its two uses. We will first examine **抜く as an independent verb**, looking at its meanings, nuances, and relationship to the kanji used to write it. 

We will then turn to **its use as an auxiliary verb**, which, from this point onward, we will consistently write as ～抜く to distinguish it from the independent verb 抜く in its basic form. 


We will therefore examine how the verb’s original meaning contributes to the development of new semantic values and what role it plays within compound verbs.


### The Verb 抜く: Meaning and Semantic Range

The verb **抜く (ぬく, nuku)** is a **transitive Group 1 verb (五段動詞 - godan)** whose basic meanings are “to extract,” “to pull out,” “to draw out,” and “to remove.” In its most concrete sense, it describes taking something out of a place where it is inserted, embedded, contained, or held, either bringing it outside or separating it from what it was part of.

This meaning is easy to recognize in expressions such as **釘を抜く (くぎをぬく)**, “to pull out a nail”; **歯を抜く (はをぬく)**, “to extract a tooth”; **刀を抜く (かたなをぬく)**, “to draw a sword”; and **栓を抜く (せんをぬく)**, “to remove a stopper.” In every case, 抜く describes an action that changes something’s position by separating it from a place where it had previously been inserted or held.

To understand this idea more fully, it is worth examining the **kanji 抜**, whose meaning is closely tied to that of the verb. The character belongs to the group of kanji containing the radical **扌 (てへん)**, an abbreviated form of 手 (て), “hand,” which often appears in characters denoting manual actions. 

The modern form 抜 derives from the traditional character **拔**, a phono-semantic compound (形声文字) consisting of 手, which indicates its semantic field, and 犮 (ハツ), which supplied its phonetic element. This formation reflects the character’s original meaning of **extracting something by hand**.

Many related meanings developed from this semantic core, not all of which involve extracting a solid object. **抜く can also refer to removing something from inside a container or system**, as in タイヤの空気を抜く (タイヤのくうきをぬく), “to let the air out of a tire,” or 水を抜く (みずをぬく), “to drain water.” What these uses have in common is the separation of something from its original location or condition.

A further extension involves **removing, omitting, or excluding an element from a larger whole**. In this sense, 抜く appears in expressions such as 朝食を抜く (ちょうしょくをぬく), “to skip breakfast,” and 文章から一文を抜く (ぶんしょうからいちぶんをぬく), “to remove a sentence from a text.” 

The same verb can also mean “to select” or “to extract a portion from a larger whole,” as when a passage is chosen from a work. These uses no longer involve physically removing something, but rather **separating an element from the whole to which it belongs**, preserving a clear connection with the original meaning.

This sense also appears in several common figurative expressions. **力を抜く (ちからをぬく)** means “to relax one’s muscles” or “to release tension,” whereas **気を抜く (きをぬく)** refers to letting one’s attention or vigilance slip. 

**手を抜く (てをぬく)** is somewhat different: it means “to cut corners” or “to do something carelessly,” omitting some of the effort or steps the task requires. In each expression, something is notionally taken away, whether tension, attention, or effort.

Beyond extraction and removal, 抜く has another important group of meanings related to **moving past a position, obstacle, or limit**. The verb can mean “to overtake,” “to surpass,” or “to go beyond,” as in 前の車を抜く (まえのくるまをぬく), “to overtake the car in front.” 

This sense also extends figuratively to standing out from others or reaching a higher level, as in **群を抜く (ぐんをぬく)**, “to stand head and shoulders above the rest” or “to be outstanding within a group.”

Closely related is the idea of **passing or cutting all the way through something**, as in 山を抜いてトンネルを造る (やまをぬいてトンネルをつくる), “to build a tunnel through a mountain.” Here, the emphasis is not so much on removing an element as on passing through a space or crossing a boundary.

This semantic range is also reflected in words containing the kanji 抜. Examples include **抜歯 (ばっし)**, “tooth extraction”; **選抜 (せんばつ)**, “selection”; and **抜群 (ばつぐん)**, “outstanding” or “far superior.” Although these words have different meanings, they show how the character can convey both extraction and separation, as well as distinction and superiority.

Overall, the different uses of 抜く can be traced to two broad conceptual images: **removing something from a position or a larger whole** and **moving beyond a position, obstacle, or limit**. 

We should not, however, assume that every use of the verb necessarily involves any particular effort or overcoming a difficulty: in many contexts, 抜く simply describes extraction, omission, or separation.

This distinction matters because it allows us to recognize the verb’s different meanings without reducing them to a single English equivalent. At the same time, it provides a basis for understanding how some of these ideas may be reinterpreted when 抜く is no longer used on its own, but becomes part of another verb.


### 抜く as an Auxiliary Verb: Meaning and Usage

When **抜く (ぬく, nuku)** is used as an auxiliary verb, its meaning moves away from the concrete idea of “extracting” or “removing” something and takes on a more abstract sense connected to **how an action is sustained or carried through to the end, or how a state reaches a particular degree of intensity**. 

This development is not entirely detached from the verb’s original meaning, however: the shift from independent verb to auxiliary retains a connection with the idea of crossing a boundary, overcoming resistance, or reaching an extreme point.

As we have seen, 抜く can describe overcoming a physical obstacle or overtaking something ahead of us. In its auxiliary use, this image is transferred to actions and experiences: **what is overcome need no longer be a physical obstacle, but may instead be a difficulty, some form of resistance, a series of obstacles, or a limit beyond which the action is carried through to completion**. 

The main verb retains its core meaning, while ～抜く introduces an additional perspective, emphasizing perseverance, completeness, or intensity.

One of ～抜く’s most characteristic meanings is **continuing an action until it is finished, without stopping despite difficulties**. Here, the compound does more than say that something is done or that an activity comes to an end: it suggests that the action is sustained all the way through, overcoming circumstances that might otherwise have hindered or interrupted it. Perseverance, determination, and endurance therefore play a central role in interpreting the compound.

A telling example is **守り抜く (まもりぬく)**, formed from 守る (まもる), “to protect” or “to defend.” Whereas the simple verb describes the act of protecting something, 守り抜く emphasizes continuing to protect it to the very end, even in the face of difficulties or threats.

*<p class="jp-example">困難な状況でも、彼女は最後まで自分の信念を守り抜いた。</p>*

*Even in difficult circumstances, she stood by her convictions to the very end.*

In this sentence, ～抜く does not merely tell us that the woman defended her convictions; it highlights the fact that **she continued to do so without giving in**, despite adverse circumstances. The compound therefore conveys both the action expressed by 守る and the fact that it is sustained all the way to the end.

A second, closely related meaning involves **the thoroughness or depth with which an action is carried out, without stopping at a superficial level**. In such cases, ～抜く does not necessarily emphasize external obstacles, but rather the degree of effort or depth with which an activity is pursued. 

The limit reached does not always correspond to the chronological end of the action; it may instead be the point beyond which there is nothing significant left to examine or explore.

This is the case with **考え抜く (かんがえぬく)**, “to think something through thoroughly” or “to examine an issue in depth.” Compared with 考える (かんがえる), which refers more generally to thinking or reflecting, the compound suggests careful consideration that continues until the issue has been fully examined.

*<p class="jp-example">彼は何日も考え抜いた末に、ようやく答えを出した。</p>*

*After thinking it through for days, he finally came up with an answer.*

Here, ～抜く highlights the thoroughness of the thought process. Rather than merely saying that the person spent a long time thinking, the speaker presents his reasoning as a process carried through thoroughly, without settling for a superficial assessment.

**Perseverance and thoroughness are not necessarily two separate meanings**: they often represent complementary aspects of the same action. An activity may be completed precisely because it is pursued with determination, just as it may be explored in depth because the person undertaking it keeps going without stopping. 

Which nuance comes to the fore depends on the meaning of the main verb and the context in which the compound is used.

Alongside these uses, ～抜く can also convey **the attainment of an extreme degree of intensity, particularly with regard to psychological conditions, feelings, or states experienced by the subject**. 

In these cases, there need not be a deliberate action to complete or a difficulty successfully overcome. Instead, the emphasis falls on how intensely a particular condition is experienced, to the point of reaching an exceptionally high level.

This meaning appears in compounds such as **困り抜く (こまりぬく)**, “to be in an extremely difficult position” or “to be completely at a loss,” and **苦しみ抜く (くるしみぬく)**, “to suffer intensely.” Here, ～抜く emphasizes not the ability to overcome adversity, but an extreme degree of distress or suffering.

*<p class="jp-example">どうしたらいいか分からず、彼は困り抜いていた。</p>*

*At a complete loss as to what to do, he was utterly overwhelmed by the situation.*

Here, **困り抜く does not mean “to manage to overcome difficulties”**. Rather, it expresses a state of profound distress in which the person faces a situation so problematic that he no longer knows how to deal with it. The image of reaching a limit is still present, but it applies to the intensity of the experience, not to the completion of an action.

This explains why **the meaning of ～抜く cannot be captured by a single English translation**: depending on the verb it combines with, it may correspond to expressions such as **“to see something through,” “to persevere,” “to carry something to completion,” “to do something thoroughly,” or “to experience a particular condition with extreme intensity”**. 

These translations are not interchangeable: each reflects a different semantic development of the auxiliary.

It is also important not to confuse how long an action lasts with the meaning conveyed by ～抜く. **The auxiliary does not simply indicate that something goes on for a long time**. Duration may matter, **but what it foregrounds is reaching a limit**: completing an activity, carrying it out exhaustively, or experiencing a state at an extreme level of intensity. 

A relatively brief action may therefore be presented as carried through to the end, whereas a prolonged state may be described without ～抜く if the speaker does not wish to emphasize this particular meaning.

Finally, it is worth stressing that **～抜く (as we will see again later) cannot be attached indiscriminately to any Japanese verb**. Whether a compound can be formed also depends on the semantic compatibility between the action or state expressed by the main verb and the meanings introduced by the auxiliary. 

Some verbs naturally lend themselves to interpretations involving perseverance or completion; others allow an extreme degree of intensity to be expressed; still others do not produce natural or commonly accepted combinations. This is therefore not simply an intensifier that can be freely attached to any action.

Moreover, not every compound verb ending in 抜く necessarily has the auxiliary meaning described above. In some formations, the second element retains a lexical meaning closer to that of the independent verb. **Identifying the role that 抜く actually plays is therefore essential to interpreting the compound correctly**, distinguishing constructions in which it expresses perseverance, thoroughness, or intensity from those in which it helps form a different lexical meaning.

We will return to these distinctions in the next section, on the formation and grammatical structure of verbs ending in ～抜く, where we will also examine which classes of verbs can combine with the auxiliary and what restrictions govern its use.

## ～抜く: Formation and Grammatical Structure

Grammatically, **～抜く (～ぬく, nuku)** is a verbal element that attaches to another verb to form a **compound verb (複合動詞, ふくごうどうし)**. The construction follows a Japanese pattern in which two verbal elements combine directly, without particles or other connecting forms.

Within the compound, the first verb expresses the basic action or state, while ～抜く changes how it is understood by introducing the meanings of perseverance, completion, or intensity discussed in the previous section. Together, the two elements form a single verbal unit that can be conjugated and used in a sentence like any other Japanese verb.

### Formation Rule: 連用形 + 抜く

Compound verbs with ～抜く follow a specific formation rule: **the auxiliary attaches to the main verb’s 連用形 (れんようけい, ren’yōkei)**, commonly known as the *ます stem (masu stem)*.

In practice, this is the same formation pattern we have already examined with other auxiliary verbs, such as the [auxiliary verb ～切る (きる, kiru)](/en/articles/kiru-auxiliary-verb-meaning-usage-japanese/) and the [auxiliary verb ～合う (あう, au)](/en/articles/au-auxiliary-verb-japanese-compound-verbs/).

The structure is therefore:

**Verb in 連用形 + 抜く → compound verb**

To obtain this stem, take the verb’s polite form and remove ます. This procedure works for both Group 1 (五段動詞) and Group 2 (一段動詞 - ichidan) verbs, while irregular verbs have their own specific forms.

Consider a few examples:

- **やる → やります → やり + 抜く → やり抜く**: to see something through without giving up.
- **守る → 守ります → 守り + 抜く → 守り抜く**: to protect or defend something to the very end.
- **考える → 考えます → 考え + 抜く → 考え抜く**: to think something through thoroughly, to examine it in depth.
- **生きる → 生きます → 生き + 抜く → 生き抜く**: to survive, to keep living despite adversity.
- **悩む → 悩みます → 悩み + 抜く → 悩み抜く**: to agonize over a problem, to wrestle with it intensely.

The same principle can also apply to verbs formed with **する**, whose 連用形 is **し**. For example, 考察する (こうさつする), “to examine” or “to analyze,” can form 考察し抜く (こうさつしぬく), “to examine an issue exhaustively.” Here too, however, whether the combination is acceptable depends on the semantic compatibility of the two elements.

It is important to note that **～抜く attaches directly to the 連用形, not to the verb’s て-form**. Thus, a construction such as 考え抜く should not be confused with a sequence of verbs linked by て, as happens with auxiliaries such as ～しまう, ～おく, or ～みる. Although both patterns add meaning to the main verb, they differ in grammatical structure.

### The Compound as a Single Verbal Unit

Once formed, the compound behaves grammatically as a single verb. The element that determines its conjugation is **抜く**, a Group 1 verb (五段動詞) that follows the カ行 (ka-gyō) conjugation pattern.

This means that, regardless of which group the main verb belongs to, the resulting compound is conjugated as a Group 1 verb.

Take **考え抜く**, formed from 考える, a Group 2 verb, and 抜く, a Group 1 verb. Its main forms are:

- **考え抜く**: plain form.
- **考え抜きます**: polite form.
- **考え抜いた**: past form.
- **考え抜いて**: て-form.
- **考え抜かない**: negative form.
- **考え抜こう**: volitional form.
- **考え抜ける**: potential form.

As we can see, 考え remains unchanged, while all the grammatical changes occur in 抜く. The same rule applies to other compounds: 守り抜く becomes 守り抜いた in the past tense, 生き抜く becomes 生き抜きます in the polite form, and やり抜く becomes やり抜かない in the negative.


We must, however, distinguish between **the sense of completion conveyed by the auxiliary and the actual completion of the action**. For example, やり抜いた presents an action as having been carried through to the end, whereas やり抜きたい expresses only the desire to see it through. Similarly, やり抜けなかった indicates that the person was unable to complete it. 

The auxiliary’s meaning remains recognizable, but the action need not actually have been completed.

Another important feature concerns the relationship between the compound and the other elements of the sentence. Although 抜く is transitive when used independently, **its presence as an auxiliary does not automatically make the compound transitive**. Argument structure—that is, the relationship between the verb, its subject, and any complements—depends primarily on the main verb.

Thus, **守り抜く** retains the transitivity of 守る, as in 約束を守り抜く (やくそくをまもりぬく), “to keep a promise to the very end.” By contrast, **生き抜く** retains the intransitive behavior of 生きる, while **悩み抜く** retains that of 悩む.

The auxiliary therefore modifies the meaning of the event without necessarily imposing the syntactic structure of the independent verb 抜く on the compound.

### Which Verbs Can Combine with ～抜く?

The 連用形 rule explains how these compounds are formed, but **it does not mean that ～抜く can be freely attached to any verb**.

Whether the auxiliary can be used depends above all on the meaning of the main verb and on the compatibility between the action or state it expresses and the semantic values introduced by ～抜く.

When the compound expresses **perseverance or completion**, the first verb generally denotes an activity that can be pursued, sustained, or carried through to a particular limit. These are often voluntary actions in which the subject can strive toward a goal, even when doing so involves difficulty or resistance.

Verbs such as **戦う (たたかう)**, “to fight”; **守る (まもる)**, “to protect”; **耐える (たえる)**, “to endure”; and **考える (かんがえる)**, “to reflect,” lend themselves to this interpretation because they describe activities that may require persistence, effort, or thoroughness.

These verbs form compounds such as 戦い抜く, 守り抜く, 耐え抜く, and 考え抜く, in which the auxiliary can emphasize carrying the action through to its endpoint.

Not every verb expressing a voluntary action, however, has the properties needed for this construction. **A brief, ordinary action, or one that does not involve any meaningful process to see through, does not necessarily lend itself to ～抜く**. 

For instance, combinations such as 野菜を炒め抜く, derived from 野菜を炒める, “to stir-fry vegetables,” or ごみを捨て抜く, from ごみを捨てる, “to throw out the trash,” would normally sound unnatural: without some special context, there is no process of perseverance or overcoming obstacles to highlight.

This does not mean that duration alone is the decisive factor. An activity may last a long time without naturally taking ～抜く, whereas a relatively brief action may take the auxiliary if understood as an undertaking pursued to the very end. **What matters is not only how long the action lasts, but how it is conceptualized**.

The situation changes when ～抜く expresses **an extreme degree of intensity**. Here, the first verb may describe not a deliberate action but a psychological condition or a state experienced by the subject.

This occurs with **困る (こまる)**, “to be in difficulty”; **悩む (なやむ)**, “to be troubled by a problem”; and **苦しむ (くるしむ)**, “to suffer.” Their respective compounds 困り抜く, 悩み抜く, and 苦しみ抜く can portray these conditions as exceptionally intense or as reaching an extreme degree.

In these uses, **the state expressed by the verb need not be under the subject’s voluntary control**. The auxiliary does not convey a decision to persevere, but rather the intensity reached by the experience.

This distinction prevents us from formulating an overly rigid rule according to which ～抜く can combine only with volitional verbs. Volition is particularly relevant in constructions expressing perseverance, but it is not a universal requirement across all uses of the auxiliary.

### Auxiliary Compounds and Lexical Compounds

One final distinction concerns the nature of verbs ending in 抜く themselves.

In Japanese linguistics, a distinction can be drawn between **syntactic compounds (統語的複合動詞)** and **lexical compounds (語彙的複合動詞)**.

In the former, ～抜く has a relatively recognizable auxiliary function and contributes to the meaning of the main verb through completion, perseverance, or intensity. Examples include 考え抜く, 守り抜く, and 悩み抜く.

In lexical compounds, by contrast, 抜く makes a semantic contribution closer to its independent meanings, or helps form a word with a specific, conventional meaning. In **引き抜く (ひきぬく)**, “to pull out,” it retains the concrete sense of extraction; **追い抜く (おいぬく)**, “to overtake,” contains the idea of moving past something; and in **見抜く (みぬく)**, “to discern” or “to see through appearances,” that idea takes on a figurative, lexicalized meaning.

The distinction does not depend on the compound’s surface form, since both types use the 連用形 of the first verb. Rather, **the semantic and grammatical relationship between the two elements** allows us to recognize whether 抜く is functioning primarily as an auxiliary or as a lexical component.

In general, auxiliary constructions allow greater freedom of combination, provided the result is semantically natural. Lexical compounds, by contrast, tend to involve more specific relationships between their elements, established through usage.

This distinction completes our account of the formation rules: **the 連用形 + 抜く structure is necessary to form the compound, but it is not enough to determine its meaning or guarantee that it sounds natural**. We must always consider the nature of the main verb, the meaning 抜く takes on, and how the resulting combination is actually used in Japanese.

## Common Verbs That Combine with ～抜く

Many verbs can combine with ～抜く, but some compounds are especially representative of the auxiliary’s different meanings. The following selection brings together forms attested in Japanese usage, grouped according to their predominant semantic nuance.

**Verbs expressing perseverance and completion**

In this group, ～抜く emphasizes sustaining an action and carrying it through to the end, often despite difficulties, obstacles, or unfavorable circumstances.

- **やり抜く (やりぬく)** — From やる, “to do”: to see something through, to complete an undertaking without giving up.
- **生き抜く (いきぬく)** — From 生きる, “to live”: to survive, to keep living through difficult circumstances.
- **守り抜く (まもりぬく)** — From 守る, “to protect” or “to defend”: to protect something to the very end, without giving in.
- **戦い抜く (たたかいぬく)** — From 戦う, “to fight”: to keep fighting until the end, despite adversity.
- **耐え抜く (たえぬく)** — From 耐える, “to bear” or “to withstand”: to endure a difficult situation until the end, holding firm without giving in.
- **信じ抜く (しんじぬく)** — From 信じる, “to believe” or “to trust”: to keep believing in someone or something to the end, without losing faith.
- **頑張り抜く (がんばりぬく)** — From 頑張る, “to work hard” or “to hang in there”: to persevere until the end, continuing to make an effort despite difficulties.
- **粘り抜く (ねばりぬく)** — From 粘る, “to persist” or “to refuse to give in”: to hold out tenaciously until the end, maintaining one’s position or commitment.

**Verbs expressing depth and thoroughness**

In these combinations, ～抜く primarily highlights the depth or completeness achieved in an activity, presenting it as something carried out carefully and exhaustively.

- **考え抜く (かんがえぬく)** — From 考える, “to think” or “to reflect”: to think something through thoroughly, to examine an issue carefully.
- **調べ抜く (しらべぬく)** — From 調べる, “to examine” or “to investigate”: to investigate thoroughly, checking every detail of an issue.
- **知り抜く (しりぬく)** — From 知る, “to know”: to know someone or something inside out, to understand every aspect of them.
- **鍛え抜く (きたえぬく)** — From 鍛える, “to train” or “to toughen”: to train or prepare someone or something with exceptional rigor and thoroughness.
- **磨き抜く (みがきぬく)** — From 磨く, “to polish” or “to refine”: to polish or refine something with the utmost care, bringing it to an exceptionally high level of quality.

**Verbs expressing extreme intensity**

In this final group, ～抜く highlights the attainment of an especially high degree of intensity. It does not necessarily describe a voluntary action, but may also refer to psychological conditions or emotional states.

- **困り抜く (こまりぬく)** — From 困る, “to be in a difficult situation”: to find oneself in an extremely difficult situation, to be completely at a loss.
- **悩み抜く (なやみぬく)** — From 悩む, “to agonize” or “to worry”: to be deeply tormented by a problem, to wrestle with it to the point of exhaustion.
- **苦しみ抜く (くるしみぬく)** — From 苦しむ, “to suffer”: to suffer intensely, to go through a period of profound suffering.
- **弱り抜く (よわりぬく)** — From 弱る, “to grow weak” or “to become debilitated”: to be in a state of extreme weakness or utter exhaustion.
- **悲しみ抜く (かなしみぬく)** — From 悲しむ, “to be sad” or “to grieve”: to experience exceptionally intense emotional pain, to be deeply grief-stricken.

These groups are not rigidly separate categories. A single compound may convey overlapping nuances: for example, **悩み抜く** can emphasize both the intensity of the anguish and the depth and duration of the reflection that accompanies it. The meaning actually conveyed therefore also depends on context.

It is also important to distinguish these compounds from other verbs ending in 抜く, such as **見抜く (みぬく)**, “to discern”; **引き抜く (ひきぬく)**, “to pull out”; and **追い抜く (おいぬく)**, “to overtake.” Although they share the same formal structure, these belong primarily to the category of lexical compounds, in which 抜く retains or develops meanings different from its specifically auxiliary senses of perseverance, thoroughness, and intensity.

The list is therefore not exhaustive, but it helps us recognize some of the most representative combinations and, more importantly, understand what makes ～抜く sound natural in a particular compound. To interpret one correctly, identifying its two constituent verbs is not enough: we must also recognize **which aspect of the first verb’s meaning the auxiliary brings to the fore and what additional nuance emerges from their combination**.

## Differences in Meaning Between the Base Verb and Its ～抜く Compound

The difference between a Japanese verb in its basic form and its corresponding **～抜く (～ぬく, nuku)** compound is not merely a matter of how intense the action is, but above all of **how the speaker presents and interprets that action**. The simple verb expresses an action or state without necessarily specifying how it unfolds, how long it lasts, how deeply it is pursued, or what difficulties are encountered. 

Adding ～抜く, by contrast, introduces a particular perspective, which may emphasize the perseverance required to sustain the action, the attainment of a final limit, or an especially high degree of intensity.

It is important to establish from the outset that **the base verb does not necessarily describe an incomplete, superficial, or effortless action**. A verb such as やる, “to do,” can describe an activity that has been fully completed, just as 考える, “to reflect,” can refer to extremely thorough reasoning. 

Those qualities, however, are not necessarily encoded in the meaning of the simple verb: they may emerge from context or be specified through other expressions.

With ～抜く, by contrast, a particular interpretation becomes an integral part of the compound’s meaning. Rather than simply reporting that an action occurred, the speaker chooses to **present it as something carried through to the end, pursued despite difficulties, or developed to an exceptionally high degree**.

This difference becomes especially clear when we compare pairs of verbs used in the same situations.

Let us begin with **やる (yaru)** and **やり抜く (yarinuku)**.

*<p class="jp-example">彼はその仕事をやった。</p>*

*He did that job.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼はその仕事をやり抜いた。</p>*

*He saw that job through without giving up.*

In the first sentence, やった tells us that the subject did a particular job. **It does not tell us whether the work required any special effort**, whether difficulties arose, or whether the person had to persevere to finish it.

In the second sentence, **やり抜いた presents the work as an undertaking carried through to the end**, suggesting that the subject remained committed despite possible obstacles or unfavorable circumstances. The distinction is therefore not simply between unfinished and completed work: やった can also refer to a job that was finished. 

What sets やり抜いた apart is its emphasis on the perseverance and determination with which the work was approached.

A similar relationship holds between **守る (mamoru)**, “to protect” or “to defend,” and **守り抜く (mamorinuku)**, “to protect or defend something to the very end.”

*<p class="jp-example">彼女は自分の信念を守った。</p>*

*She defended her convictions.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼女は自分の信念を守り抜いた。</p>*

*She stood by her convictions to the very end, without giving in.*

Both sentences describe defending one’s convictions. With 守った, however, the focus remains on the fact that the convictions were defended. **守り抜いた adds the idea of continuing that defense to the very end**, even in the face of pressure, difficulties, or attempts to make her change her mind.

The auxiliary does not change what is being defended; it changes how the speaker portrays the action, from a simple act of defense to conduct characterized by persistence, steadfastness, and resilience.

The contrast takes on a slightly different character when ～抜く combines with verbs describing intellectual activity. Consider **考える (kangaeru)**, “to think” or “to reflect,” and **考え抜く (kangaenuku)**, “to think something through thoroughly” or “to examine an issue in depth.”

*<p class="jp-example">彼はその問題について考えた。</p>*

*He thought about that problem.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼はその問題について考え抜いた。</p>*

*He examined that problem thoroughly, carefully considering every aspect of it.*

In the first sentence, 考えた indicates that the subject gave the problem some thought. On its own, however, the sentence does not tell us how deeply he considered it.

The second sentence, by contrast, highlights **the depth and thoroughness of his reasoning**. 考え抜いた suggests that the person examined the issue with particular care and effort, trying to consider it fully rather than settling for a superficial assessment.

Here, the contribution of ～抜く does not necessarily involve overcoming external obstacles, but rather reaching a high degree of thoroughness. It is also important to note that **thinking something through does not necessarily mean finding a solution**. Someone may have thought an issue through thoroughly (考え抜いた), examining it carefully, without arriving at a satisfactory answer. The auxiliary characterizes the process of reflection; it does not automatically guarantee that the goal that motivated the reflection has been achieved.

An even sharper distinction appears between **生きる (ikiru)**, “to live,” and **生き抜く (ikinuku)**, “to survive” or “to keep living despite adversity.”

*<p class="jp-example">彼は厳しい時代を生きた。</p>*

*He lived in a difficult era.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼は厳しい時代を生き抜いた。</p>*

*He managed to survive those difficult times, overcoming the hardships they brought.*

In the first sentence, 生きた describes the fact that the person lived during a particular historical period. The adjective 厳しい (きびしい), “harsh” or “difficult,” suggests that living conditions were unfavorable, but the verb itself remains focused on the experience of living.

With 生き抜いた, by contrast, **surviving hardship becomes an essential part of the meaning**. The person is portrayed as someone who managed to stay alive through adverse circumstances. The action expressed by 生きる is not simply intensified: it is reinterpreted as a process of endurance and overcoming adversity.

So far, we have considered compounds in which ～抜く primarily emphasizes perseverance, completion, or thoroughness. The contrast with the simple verb changes considerably, however, when the first element describes **a psychological state or a condition that is not necessarily under the subject’s voluntary control**.

A telling example is the pair **困る (komaru)** and **困り抜く (komarinuku)**.

*<p class="jp-example">彼はどうすればいいか分からず、困っていた。</p>*

*Not knowing what to do, he was in a difficult position.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼はどうすればいいか分からず、困り抜いていた。</p>*

*At a complete loss as to what to do, he found himself in an extremely difficult situation.*

In the first sentence, 困っていた indicates that the person was facing a problem and did not know how to deal with it.

In the second, **困り抜いていた presents that difficulty as especially severe, almost at the limits of what the person can bear or do about it**. Adding ～抜く does not suggest that the person deliberately persevered in being troubled, or that he managed to resolve the situation. Rather, it indicates that his state had reached an extreme level.

This example clearly shows why it would be misleading to assign ～抜く the blanket meaning “to finish doing something.” In compounds expressing psychological states, the idea of reaching a limit can concern **the intensity of the condition**, rather than the completion of an action.

The same principle appears in the contrast between **苦しむ (kurushimu)**, “to suffer,” and **苦しみ抜く (kurushiminuku)**, “to suffer intensely” or “to go through extreme suffering.”

*<p class="jp-example">彼は長い間、病気に苦しんだ。</p>*

*He suffered from the illness for a long time.*

*<p class="jp-example">彼は長い間、病気に苦しみ抜いた。</p>*

*He went through a long period of extreme suffering because of the illness.*

Both sentences indicate that the subject suffered over an extended period. The expression 長い間 (ながいあいだ), “for a long time,” makes the duration of the condition explicit in both cases.

The difference lies elsewhere. **苦しみ抜いた presents the suffering as experienced with extreme intensity**, whereas 苦しんだ, though it may describe an equally painful experience, does not necessarily encode this particular nuance. Once again, ～抜く changes how the condition is portrayed, without implying that the person voluntarily chose to remain in it or managed to overcome it.

These comparisons reveal a fundamental principle: **～抜く does not always add the same information to the main verb**. With some verbs, its contribution primarily concerns sustaining an action until the end; with others, the depth or thoroughness of a process; and with still others, the attainment of an extreme degree of intensity.

The difference from the simple form therefore depends on the relationship between the meaning of the first verb and that of the auxiliary. We must also avoid confusing the meaning of ～抜く with whether the action is actually completed. **やり抜いた** presents an activity as completed, but **やり抜きたい** expresses only the desire to accomplish this, while **やり抜けなかった** indicates that the subject was unable to see it through. 

The auxiliary’s meaning remains recognizable, but whether the action is actually completed depends on the grammatical form and the context.

Ultimately, **the base verb identifies the action or state, while the ～抜く compound offers a more specific interpretation of it**, highlighting a limit reached, a process pursued with perseverance, or an extreme degree of depth or intensity. 

It is this different perspective, more than any simple difference in expressive force, that distinguishes the two types of verb and explains why they are not always interchangeable, even when they describe the same situation.

## ～抜く: Register and Usage Contexts

The auxiliary **～抜く (～ぬく, nuku)** is part of contemporary Japanese and can be used in both speech and writing. **It is not restricted to any particularly formal, literary, or colloquial register, nor does it convey politeness in itself**. 

Its defining feature is the expressive force it lends to the action or state being described, highlighting **perseverance, thoroughness, or intensity**.

This makes ～抜く especially well suited to contexts in which speakers want to stress the effort involved in dealing with a situation, the determination to pursue a goal, or the ability to see something through. It is therefore natural to encounter compounds such as **やり抜く**, **頑張り抜く**, **戦い抜く**, and **耐え抜く** in discussions of work, study, sporting competitions, personal challenges, or experiences requiring persistence and endurance.

In such contexts, the auxiliary can give an expression a resolute, motivational, or even solemn tone, particularly when used to declare an intention, make a promise, or urge someone not to give up.

*<p class="jp-example">どんな困難があっても、最後までやり抜きます。</p>*

*Whatever difficulties arise, I will see it through to the end without giving up.*

The compound **やり抜きます** does not make the sentence polite simply by containing ～抜く: its level of politeness comes from the ～ます ending. The auxiliary, rather, gives the statement the force of a firm commitment, emphasizing the speaker’s intention to continue until the task is complete despite any obstacles.

Expressions of this kind also suit public speeches, statements of intent, professional presentations, and institutional communications intended to convey resolve. Nevertheless, **the presence of ～抜く does not automatically make a sentence formal**: the same compound can appear in a conversation between friends, taking on a more spontaneous, encouraging tone.

*<p class="jp-example">ここまで頑張ったんだから、最後までやり抜こう！</p>*

*We’ve worked so hard to get this far—let’s see it through to the end without giving up!*

Here, the informal context and the volitional ～よう form combine to create a direct, familiar exhortation. The meaning of ～抜く remains essentially the same, however: what is being encouraged is not merely continuing the activity, but the determination needed to carry it through to the end.

The auxiliary is also widely used in **narratives and accounts of personal experience**, particularly when the focus is on the difficulties someone has faced or the journey they have been through. Verbs such as 生き抜く, 守り抜く, and 戦い抜く allow events to be portrayed in terms of endurance, persistence, or overcoming adversity. This makes them especially effective in stories, personal accounts, biographies, and news articles.

In these contexts, ～抜く often helps present an experience as a meaningful journey rather than merely recounting a sequence of events. The tone may become emotionally powerful, but not necessarily exaggerated or artificial: much depends on the nature of the experience and how the compound is woven into the narrative.

The case is different with verbs expressing **depth and thoroughness**, such as 考え抜く and 調べ抜く. These compounds also suit professional, academic, or analytical contexts in which the speaker wishes to emphasize the care taken in reflection, investigation, or decision-making. Here, ～抜く does not necessarily communicate strong emotion; instead, it may highlight the seriousness and rigor with which an activity was undertaken.

*<p class="jp-example">何度も議論し、考え抜いた末に、この方針を決定しました。</p>*

*After extensive discussion and careful consideration of every aspect, we decided to adopt this course of action.*

Here, **考え抜いた** presents the decision as the outcome of particularly thorough deliberation. The construction fits a professional register and helps convey the idea that the choice was not made lightly.

When ～抜く expresses **an extreme degree of intensity**, as in 困り抜く, 苦しみ抜く, and 弱り抜く, the tone can become more distinctly descriptive or emotional. Such expressions are particularly effective when portraying the severity of a psychological or physical condition or conveying to readers or listeners how intensely someone has experienced a particular situation.

In these uses, the auxiliary does not necessarily convey admiration or determination. **～抜く has no inherently positive emotional connotation**: it can describe both the perseverance of someone who bravely faces adversity and the intensity of suffering or the persistence of negative behavior. 

Compounds such as いじめ抜く, “to bully or mistreat someone relentlessly,” clearly show that pushing an action to an extreme does not necessarily imply a favorable judgment of the subject.

One final consideration concerns **whether the auxiliary sounds natural and is appropriate to the intended meaning**. Precisely because ～抜く introduces a distinctive semantic nuance, it is not always suitable when the aim is simply to describe an ordinary action in a neutral way. 

For example, to say that a job has been finished, it may be enough to use 終わる or 終える, or the main verb in whatever form best suits the context. Using ～抜く, by contrast, means choosing to foreground an additional aspect of the experience, such as the effort involved, the overcoming of difficulties, or the exceptional care with which the activity was completed.

This greater expressive force should not, however, be taken to mean that ～抜く is invariably solemn or dramatic. **The intensity perceived depends on the particular compound and the communicative situation**. A common, established verb such as 考え抜く can fit naturally into an everyday conversation, whereas a declaration such as 必ず守り抜く, “to protect something at all costs, to the very end,” may carry a much stronger sense of commitment.

In short, **～抜く does not indicate a particular level of formality; it introduces a semantic nuance that can significantly affect the tone of an utterance**. It sounds natural when the speaker wishes to portray an action or state as reaching a meaningful limit, whether that limit marks the completion of an undertaking, the thoroughness with which an activity is carried out, or an extreme degree of intensity. 

Recognizing this nuance not only helps us understand ～抜く compounds correctly, but also enables us to judge when they are more expressive than the simple verb.

## ～抜く: Usage Examples

*<p class="jp-example">試験まであと一週間しかないけれど、自分で立てた勉強計画は最後までやり抜くつもりだ。</p>*

*There is only one week left until the exam, but I’m determined to stick to the study plan I made and see it through to the end.*

*やり抜く emphasizes the determination to complete an activity despite difficulties. The ～つもりだ form expresses the intention to do so.*

*<p class="jp-example">周りに何を言われても、彼女は自分の信念を守り抜いた。</p>*

*No matter what anyone else said to her, she stood by her convictions to the very end.*

*守り抜く presents the defense of one’s convictions as a firm, sustained commitment that does not yield to outside pressure.*

*<p class="jp-example">チームは主力選手を欠きながらも、最後まで戦い抜いた。</p>*

*Despite being without some of its key players, the team fought until the very end.*

*戦い抜く emphasizes perseverance in a fight or competition, regardless of the final outcome.*

*<p class="jp-example">厳しい冬を耐え抜いた人々に、ようやく春が訪れた。</p>*

*For those who had endured a bitter winter, spring had finally arrived.*

*耐え抜く highlights the ability to withstand a difficult situation until the end without being overcome by adversity.*

*<p class="jp-example">戦争を生き抜いた祖母は、平和の大切さを何度も話してくれた。</p>*

*My grandmother, who survived the war, often told me how important peace was.*

*生き抜く means more than simply living: it means managing to stay alive through exceptionally difficult circumstances.*

*<p class="jp-example">何日も考え抜いたが、結局、納得できる答えは見つからなかった。</p>*

*I thought it through for days, but in the end I couldn’t find an answer I was satisfied with.*

*考え抜く expresses particularly thorough reflection. It does not necessarily imply that the problem is resolved.*

*<p class="jp-example">記者は資料を調べ抜き、事件の背景にある問題を明らかにした。</p>*

*The journalist examined the documents thoroughly, shedding light on the issues underlying the case.*

*調べ抜く highlights the completeness and accuracy of the investigation, presenting it as an exhaustive inquiry.*

*<p class="jp-example">周囲の人が疑っても、私は彼の無実を信じ抜く。</p>*

*Even if those around me have their doubts, I will continue to believe firmly in his innocence.*

*信じ抜く expresses unwavering trust maintained to the end, even when circumstances or other people’s opinions might call it into question.*

*<p class="jp-example">何度も転職を考えたが、悩み抜いた末に、今の職場に残ることにした。</p>*

*I thought about changing jobs several times, but after agonizing over the decision, I chose to stay where I am.*

*悩み抜く expresses particularly intense inner turmoil, which in this context accompanies careful consideration before making a decision.*

*<p class="jp-example">突然の出費が重なり、生活費のやりくりに困り抜いている。</p>*

*Unexpected expenses have piled up, and I’m now completely at a loss as to how to make ends meet.*

*困り抜く indicates that a state of extreme difficulty has been reached. Here, ～抜く conveys intensity, not perseverance or completion.*

## Conclusions

Our analysis of **～抜く (～ぬく, nuku)** shows that the meaning of a Japanese compound verb depends not only on how its elements combine, but also on how those elements work together to portray an action or state. The main verb still identifies what happens, while ～抜く **adds a perspective that may highlight perseverance, completion, thoroughness, or the attainment of an extreme degree of intensity**.

One particularly interesting feature is that **～抜く does not merely describe the endpoint; it can also evoke the journey required to reach it**. 

In a compound such as やり抜く, the final outcome matters precisely because the activity is portrayed as something that had to be pursued all the way through. 

With 考え抜く, by contrast, the focus is on the depth of thought involved, without necessarily implying that a solution has been reached. 

The distinction matters: bringing a process to completion does not always mean achieving the desired result.

The same principle explains why **戦い抜く does not necessarily mean winning a battle or competition**. The subject may have fought with determination to the very end and still been defeated. The auxiliary characterizes how the action is undertaken; it does not guarantee success. 

In compounds expressing extreme intensity, such as 困り抜く, there may not even be a positive outcome: what is emphasized is how extreme the condition has become.

These observations also help distinguish ～抜く from other Japanese auxiliaries expressing completion. **～切る** tends to highlight the full completion of an action or the reaching of a limit, while **～通す** places particular emphasis on sustaining the action until the end. **～抜く**, by contrast, may give greater weight to perseverance and overcoming difficulties. 

The three constructions share certain features, but they are not necessarily interchangeable: choosing one over another means highlighting different aspects of the same event.

For learners of Japanese, the main recommendation is therefore **to avoid memorizing ～抜く with a single fixed translation**, such as “to do something until the end.” That equivalent may be useful in some contexts, but it is not enough to interpret compounds such as 考え抜く or 困り抜く. A better approach is to start with the meaning of the main verb and ask what kind of limit is being emphasized: the completion of an undertaking, the difficulty of sustaining it, the depth of a process, or the intensity of a state.

A second useful approach is **to learn compounds through complete sentences rather than as mere combinations of verbs**. Context allows us to recognize not only what a word means, but also the situations in which it sounds natural. 

Comparing a sentence containing the simple verb with one using ～抜く is a particularly effective exercise, because it forces us to identify what information the auxiliary adds and what nuance would be lost if it were removed.

It is equally important **not to apply the formation rule mechanically to every verb**. The fact that a compound can be formed according to the grammatical pattern does not guarantee that Japanese speakers actually use it. 

When a combination is unfamiliar, it is advisable to check whether it exists and how it is used in dictionaries, corpora, or authentic examples—paying attention to context and frequency—before trying to incorporate it into your own usage and potentially assigning it the wrong meaning. In particular, we need to distinguish compounds in which 抜く functions as an auxiliary from those in which it retains an independent lexical meaning.

Finally, it is worth remembering that **a grammatically richer construction is not necessarily a better expressive choice**. If the aim is simply to communicate that an activity was performed or completed, the base verb may be entirely sufficient. 

～抜く becomes especially effective when we want to draw attention to something the simple form does not necessarily bring out: sustained effort, steadfast determination, a thorough investigation, or an experience of extreme intensity.

Learning to use ～抜く therefore means going beyond simply knowing how it is formed. Above all, it means **developing the ability to recognize which aspect of an action the speaker wishes to bring to the fore**, understanding not only what is being said but also why that particular compound verb has been chosen to express it.
