**What does kikkake (きっかけ) mean in Japanese, and how do you use it?**



きっかけ (written 切っ掛け in kanji) is a noun referring to **the spark, opportunity, or factor that sets an action, a change, or a new situation in motion**. It might be an encounter that sparks an interest, an experience that leads someone to change their habits, or something someone says that prompts a decision.

At its core, the word refers to whatever gets something started. Depending on the context, it can therefore be translated as “trigger,” “spark,” “starting point,” or “initial impetus.”

Consider, for example, the question **日本語を勉強し始めたきっかけは何ですか。**, *“What prompted you to start learning Japanese?”* Someone answering might mention an anime that captured their imagination, a trip to Japan, or meeting a Japanese person.

That experience is presented as the event that set their language-learning journey in motion, even if other interests or motivations also played a part in the decision.

This emphasis on beginnings is the key to understanding the nuance of きっかけ. Its meaning can overlap with **理由 (りゆう, “reason”)** and **原因 (げんいん, “cause”)**, but it focuses primarily on **what set a process in motion or marked the beginning of a new phase**.

In the example above, wanting to understand the language might explain why someone studies Japanese; a particular anime, meanwhile, might be the きっかけ that turned that interest into concrete action. The two perspectives may coincide, but they are not necessarily the same.

Even the translation “opportunity” needs to be understood in light of this function. A pleasant encounter may be the beginning of a friendship, but an illness, an accident, or an argument can also become the starting point for subsequent developments. **きっかけ does not, in itself, imply a positive or negative judgment**: it highlights the connection between what started something and what followed.

Grammatically, the word appears both as a noun in its own right—for example, in questions about what first prompted a choice—and in constructions such as **～をきっかけに（して）**, **～がきっかけで**, **～をきっかけとして**, and **～がきっかけとなって**. These patterns link the initial event to the action or change that follows, with differences in structure and nuance that we will examine in detail.

The following table summarizes the main patterns.

We will begin by exploring the meaning of きっかけ, its kanji spelling, 切っ掛け, and its historical uses. We will then look at how the various constructions are formed, including patterns involving an entire nominalized action or experience, before comparing がきっかけで and をきっかけに and explaining the role of their particles.

Finally, we will consider register and usage contexts, the relationship with 契機 (けいき, keiki), and a series of annotated examples to see how to choose and interpret these expressions in real situations.

## きっかけ (kikkake): Meaning, Origin, and Kanji



### Historical Background


きっかけ is one of those words whose modern usage becomes even clearer when we look at its history.

Its history preserves concrete and specialized meanings that shed light on how it is used today. To trace that history, it is helpful to start with the attestations recorded in historical dictionaries, distinguishing documented uses from interpretations of how those uses arose.

The **精選版 日本国語大辞典**, published by Shōgakukan, records an older sense referring to the beginning of a cut in a material, a partially cut section, or the end of an object. For this group of meanings, it cites an example from the **娵入記**, a fifteenth-century text dated approximately **1443–1473**, during the Muromachi period.

This association with cutting has a parallel in the word **切り掛け (きりかけ, kirikake)**, which dictionaries also record as meaning “to cut something only partway” or “something that has been partially cut.”

The comparison is therefore useful for identifying the physical image evoked by the word. However, the dictionary entries consulted do not explicitly describe a phonetic development from きりかけ to きっかけ. It is better not to present that derivation as an established fact based solely on the similarity between the two forms.

To understand how this imagery might relate to the modern meaning, imagine making the first cut in a material, creating a point from which the work can continue. Figuratively, an encounter, a remark, or an experience can create a similar opening: **the point at which something begins**. This image offers an intuitive way to understand the connection between the meanings, without claiming to reconstruct the word's entire historical development.

The sense of “a prompt or opportunity to begin something” is already attested in the **四座役者目録 (1646–1653)**. The dictionary also records an older sense close to “vigor,” “spirit,” or “stubborn pride” (**気勢・心意気・意地**), documented in the **甲陽軍鑑**, from the early seventeenth century.

Another important area is **theatrical usage**, with an attestation in the 舞正語磨 (1658). In Noh and Kabuki, きっかけ can refer to the cue that governs the transition to the next action: a line of dialogue, a gesture, or another signal that coordinates performers, music, and stage changes. This meaning remains in use in theatrical terminology today.

A concrete example appears in the technical documentation of the **National Theatre of Japan**: its lighting cue sheet lists きっかけ, referring to actors' movements or lines that cue lighting changes.

The manual also pairs きっかけ with the English word **cue**, a signal indicating when an action should be performed or a technical change made. The link between an initial signal and the action that follows is particularly clear here.

The dates given above establish that these meanings were present in the cited texts; they do not necessarily indicate when those meanings first arose, nor do they, by themselves, establish a definite sequence of semantic developments. Taken together, however, these uses help explain why きっかけ can evoke both a starting point and a signal that sets something in motion.

For more on the history of Japanese expressions, the site also has a dedicated article on [ご無沙汰 (gobusata)](/en/articles/gobusata-meaning-origin-history-japanese/), exploring its origins and how they relate to its modern use as a polite expression.




### Meaning of きっかけ and Kanji Analysis

**きっかけ (kikkake) is a noun referring to whatever provides the initial impetus for something or sets a change in motion**. It may be an event, an encounter, an experience, or even a single remark: something that serves as the starting point for what happens next.

Common English equivalents include **“spark,” “opportunity,” “trigger,” “starting point,” and “initial impetus”**. The best choice depends on the context: “spark” suits the emergence of an idea or interest; “opportunity” can describe a circumstance that leads someone to take up an activity; and “trigger” places greater emphasis on setting a process in motion.

The word can be written in hiragana as **きっかけ** or in kanji as **切っ掛け**. Looking at its characters brings us back to the concrete image discussed in the historical overview:

- **切** is the character used in **切る (きる, kiru)**, “to cut.” It therefore evokes cutting, making an incision, or separating something. You may also find it useful to read our discussion of the meaning of 切 in the article on [切る as an auxiliary verb](/en/articles/kiru-auxiliary-verb-meaning-usage-japanese/).

- **掛** appears in **掛ける (かける, kakeru)**, a verb with numerous meanings, including “to hang,” “to place,” and “to apply.” To understand its connection with the beginning of an action, it is especially helpful to consider its use as the second element of a compound verb: **～かける** can indicate that an action is beginning or has been initiated, as in **書きかける**, “to start writing.” The element **掛け (かけ)** is the 連用形 of 掛ける, the form that appears before ます in 掛けます.

The notion of “beginning” is therefore associated with **a particular use of ～かける in compound verbs**. The ideas of making a cut and setting an action in motion help us picture an initial point from which something can proceed. This is a useful way to remember what きっかけ means, without reducing the word to the literal sum of its characters.

In modern Japanese, **きっかけ links an initial event to something that begins or changes as a result of it**. The phrase **日本語を勉強するきっかけ**, for example, refers to the spark or opportunity that leads someone to begin studying Japanese.

For example:

日本の映画を見たことがきっかけで、日本語の勉強を始めました。
*"Watching a Japanese film was what prompted me to start studying the language."*

The film is presented as the experience that got the process of learning Japanese started. Other motivations may also have been involved, such as an interest in languages or a desire to travel: きっかけ identifies the event that, in this account, prompted that first step.

This helps clarify its relationship with **理由 (りゆう, riyuu)**. 理由 refers to the reason, motivation, or explanation for something, and it can also concern feelings, preferences, and personal circumstances. **It does not necessarily imply a logical or rational motivation**.

The difference from きっかけ is primarily one of perspective: 理由 answers the broader question of “why,” whereas きっかけ focuses on “what got things started.” The same experience can play both roles.

**原因 (げんいん, gen’in)**, “cause,” can also overlap with きっかけ, but it refers more directly to what produces an outcome or brings about a situation. With きっかけ, the emphasis falls particularly on **the beginning of a subsequent development**.

For example, rain may be the cause of a match being canceled; that cancellation may then become the きっかけ for reviewing how events are organized. In the second case, the focus is on the new initiative that arose from what happened.

きっかけ often appears in accounts of interests, decisions, and new activities, but **its meaning is not necessarily positive**. It can also introduce a deterioration in relationships or other problematic developments:

*些細なけんかがきっかけで、二人は口をきかなくなった。*
*"A minor argument led the two of them to stop speaking to each other."*

Here, the argument is the incident that set the change in their relationship in motion.

Finally, the initial impetus may arise by chance or be deliberately sought out. An unexpected meeting can become a きっかけ, but someone can also look for an opportunity to talk to another person, start an activity, or change a situation. The central idea remains the **role of that circumstance as the starting point for what follows**.

## Grammatical Structure and Formation Rules

Grammatically, きっかけ is a noun and can be used like any other noun. In the constructions we will examine, the element that serves as the starting point is generally marked by を or が, while the following clause describes the action, change, or development that results from it.

Let's look at the most common patterns:

- N + をきっかけに (して) – Presents N as the starting point of a new action, a change, or a subsequent development. Depending on the context, it can be rendered as **“with N as the starting point,”** **“N provided the impetus for…”**, or **“N triggered…”**.

  The particle を marks the element N in the construction Nをきっかけに（して）. The expression can be understood as “with N as the starting point” and can describe either an opportunity someone deliberately takes or a change that occurs without a conscious decision.

  Example: 留学をきっかけに日本文化に興味を持った。 - "Studying abroad sparked my interest in Japanese culture."

  This construction can also appear as ～をきっかけにして, with a similar meaning.

- N + をきっかけとして – A construction similar to the preceding one, but slightly more formal in register. Literally, **“taking N as the kikkake (starting point)”**.

  It functions much like をきっかけに, and the two are often interchangeable.

  Here too, N is marked by を.
	
  For example: 昨年の事故をきっかけとして、安全対策が強化された。 - "The accident last year prompted stricter safety measures."

- N + がきっかけで – Presents N as the initial event that set an action, a change, or a new situation in motion.

  It can be rendered as “It was N that set… in motion” or “N triggered…”. Renderings such as **“thanks to N…”** or **“because of N…”** depend on the context and whether the event is viewed positively or negatively.

  In this structure, が marks N as the subject of the nominal predicate Nがきっかけだ, “N is the trigger / starting point.” で is the connective form of the copula だ, allowing this information to be linked to the clause describing the subsequent development.

  Example: ある出会いがきっかけで人生が変わった。 - "One particular encounter changed my life."

  This form highlights N as the specific event that triggered what followed.




- N + がきっかけとなる / となった – A variant in which きっかけ serves as the nominal part of the predicate with なる/なった (literally, “to become [the] kikkake”).

  It is often used to say that N **“served as the kikkake”** for a particular change.

  For example: 彼の一言がきっかけとなり、みんなが行動を始めた。 - "A single remark from him prompted everyone to take action."

  This construction is close in meaning to がきっかけで and presents N as something that becomes or serves as the starting point for what follows. In きっかけとなる, と introduces the role assumed by N, and なる is the verb in the predicate. Forms such as となって and となり are used to connect the construction to a subsequent clause.

- V (plain form) + こと/の + がきっかけで – When the initial event is expressed by a verbal clause, that clause can be nominalized with こと or の and take the place of N in the construction Nがきっかけで. When recounting a past experience, the form Vた + こと/の + がきっかけで is very common.

  The choice of verb form depends on the content and temporal relationships within the sentence: ～た is not a mandatory form imposed by きっかけ. Nominalization is also used in the constructions ～をきっかけに（して） and ～をきっかけとして.

  For example: 日本へ旅行に行ったことがきっかけで、日本語の勉強を始めました。 - "A trip to Japan was what prompted me to start studying Japanese."

  Here, こと nominalizes “having traveled to Japan.”

  Similarly, ～たのがきっかけで can perform the same function. In these constructions, こと or の allows the clause to occupy the position of the element N marked by が or を. The phrase 日本語を勉強し始めたきっかけ, “what prompted someone to begin studying Japanese,” works differently: here, the clause directly modifies the noun きっかけ and does not require こと or の.

- …きっかけは～ – In addition to the patterns above, きっかけ can be used as an independent noun in copular statements or questions.

  For example, a typical question is ～きっかけは何ですか, meaning “What was the kikkake (reason/spark) for ~?”

  In this pattern, は presents きっかけ as the topic.

  One common context is asking someone what prompted them to begin doing something:

  For example: 日本語を勉強し始めたきっかけは何ですか。- "What made you decide to start learning Japanese?"

	

In a formal setting, you may also encounter 日本語の勉強を始められたきっかけは何ですか。 Here, ～られる is honorific: it expresses respect for the person who began studying. The form is identical to the one used for the passive, but in this interpretation its function is honorific, not passive.

In reply, any of the preceding patterns can be used to explain the triggering event (e.g., アニメを見たことがきっかけです – *“Watching an anime was what got me started”*).



**Note on particles**: in the constructions examined, **を marks N in ～をきっかけに（して） and ～をきっかけとして, whereas が marks N as the subject in ～がきっかけで and ～がきっかけとなる**.

The elements following きっかけ do not all belong to the same grammatical category: に and と are particles; して and なって are the ～て forms of the verbs する and なる; and で in ～がきっかけで is the connective form of the copula だ.

For example, in 失恋をきっかけに... *“heartbreak”* is presented as the starting point; in ある出来事がきっかけで... the event *“an incident”* is the subject of the nominal predicate, and で links that information to the subsequent development.

## Differences Between がきっかけで and をきっかけに

One point learners often find difficult is understanding the difference between がきっかけで and をきっかけに.



  The two constructions share the same basic meaning: both identify the element that initiated an action, a change, or a new situation. In many contexts, they can describe the very same event. The difference lies primarily in their syntax and in how they present the relationship between A and B.

In "A がきっかけで B", A is explicitly identified as the trigger for B through a nominal clause equivalent to “A is the starting point / triggering event.”

  For example: アニメがきっかけで、日本語を勉強し始めた。 - *“An anime was what got me started learning Japanese.”*

  It is natural when answering a question such as:

  何がきっかけで日本語を勉強し始めたんですか。- *“What prompted you to start learning Japanese?”*



  By contrast, in "A をきっかけに B", A is treated as the point that marks the beginning of a new phase.

  For example: 留学をきっかけに、人生観が変わった。- *“Studying abroad was a turning point that changed my outlook on life.”*

  The sense of *“starting point”* is shared by both constructions. For example, the following two sentences can describe the same event:

アニメがきっかけで、日本語を勉強し始めた。
アニメをきっかけに、日本語を勉強し始めた。

In both sentences, an anime provides the impetus to begin studying Japanese. With がきっかけで, the anime is identified as the trigger; with をきっかけに, it is presented as the starting point for the learning process. This difference in framing does not establish a necessary opposition between an involuntary change and a deliberate action.



### Why を with きっかけに but が with きっかけで?

This is another interesting grammatical point.

The construction "N をきっかけに" can be related to its extended form "N をきっかけにして" and to the pattern "N をきっかけにする".

The construction can be paraphrased as:

*“taking N as the starting point.”*

In the pattern with する, を marks N as the object, while きっかけに specifies the role in which N is being presented. This comparison helps explain the construction, but it does not mean that the change described in the sentence must have been brought about deliberately: N may simply be presented as the starting point for what followed.

By contrast, in *"N がきっかけで"*, が marks N as the subject of the nominal predicate *"N がきっかけだ"*:

*“N is the trigger / starting point.”*

で is the connective form of the copula だ, traditionally classified as its 連用形. The sequence *"N がきっかけで、B。"* therefore links the identification of the trigger to the clause describing the subsequent development:

*“N set B in motion.”*

The contrast between transitive する and intransitive なる, meanwhile, helps explain the pair をきっかけにして / がきっかけになって. With がきっかけで, however, the analysis rests on the nominal predicate with だ: explaining が does not require positing an implicit なる.

## Register and Usage Contexts of きっかけ

In terms of register, きっかけ is an exceptionally natural and versatile word.

It is neither specialized nor particularly solemn: it can appear in everyday conversation, interviews, personal essays, educational explanations, general-interest articles, or writing in a moderately formal style.

For precisely this reason, it is often the safest choice when referring to *“what set something in motion”*, whether that is an action, an interest, a change, or a new situation.

きっかけ sounds perfectly natural in personal and informal contexts. It can refer to even seemingly minor events: a meeting, something someone said, a trip, a book, an illness, a film, a lesson, or a chance experience.

For example:

日本人の友達ができたのをきっかけに、日本語を勉強し始めました。
*“I started learning Japanese after making friends with a Japanese person.”*

Here, きっかけ does not indicate a rigid or logically necessary cause. Rather, it identifies **the point at which something began**: the spark, the opportunity, or the moment that opened up a new possibility.

The word can also be used in negative or difficult situations. An accident, an illness, an argument, or a crisis can be the きっかけ for a change.

For example:

事故をきっかけに、安全対策が見直された。
*“The accident prompted a review of safety measures.”*

In this case, きっかけ does not simply mean *“cause”* in a technical sense. The accident is presented as the event that triggered a subsequent response.

きっかけ also sounds natural in writing, particularly when the tone is not overly formal. It works well in general-interest articles, grammatical explanations, interviews, and biographical accounts because きっかけ retains a concrete, human dimension.

It is well suited to discussing how an interest began, what prompted a personal choice, or what led to a change in someone's life.

## The Formal Alternative 契機 (keiki)


**契機（けいき）** is different. 契機 is not colloquial: it is more formal, more characteristic of written language, and more abstract.

It frequently appears in newspapers, essays, official documents, academic texts, political speeches, and historical analyses. Compared with きっかけ, it tends to give greater weight to the initial event, presenting it as a decisive factor or a turning point.

For example:

コロナ禍を契機に、リモートワークが広がった。
*“The pandemic marked a turning point in the spread of remote work.”*

Here, 契機 sounds more formal and analytical than きっかけ. The sentence is not simply describing a minor event in someone's personal life, but a broad social or structural change.

In short, きっかけ is the more general, natural, and everyday term. It can refer to events large or small, personal or public, positive or negative.

契機, by contrast, is more formal and is preferred when the initial event is presented as an important moment, a turning point, or the basis for a broader change.

For this reason, in an educational article, 契機 is best explained not as a colloquial variant but as a formal synonym of きっかけ used mainly in writing and especially common in journalistic, academic, institutional, or analytical contexts.

## Conclusions

The most useful principle for understanding and using きっかけ correctly is recognizing **the relationship between what set something in motion and what subsequently began or changed**. An event may explain how an interest, a decision, or a new situation originated, even when it was only one of several contributing factors. Its role as a trigger matters more than how significant it may initially seem: even a casual remark or chance encounter can mark the beginning of a new journey.

The constructions **～をきっかけに（して）** and **～がきっかけで** can often describe the same experience through different syntactic structures. For learners, it is helpful to memorize them as complete patterns and understand the function of each element. This makes it easier to choose the correct particles without assuming that every pair of sentences must differ categorically in meaning.

When creating an example, there are two things to check above all: **what is presented as the starting point and what followed from it**. Choose an experience specific enough to make the connection clear. If you express it through a verbal clause, check whether the construction requires nominalization with こと or の; when describing past experiences, the sequences ～たこと and ～たの are particularly common.

Context also guides the choice of words. きっかけ lends itself to personal accounts and explanations in an everyday register; 契機 may be more appropriate in formal writing, especially when the event is presented as a significant turning point. In either case, consider what the sentence is intended to emphasize rather than choosing an expression solely on the basis of an English gloss such as “opportunity” or “cause.”

A useful exercise is to write three short sentences about your own experiences: when you began studying Japanese, how a habit developed, and a change in your interests. For each one, identify the trigger first, then what followed from it. Next, try rewriting one sentence by replacing がきっかけで with をきっかけに while keeping the events the same. Connecting these constructions to real experiences will help you remember **their meaning and form together with the role they play in recounting events**.
